Section A: Figural Reasoning

Q1: C. Notch now on the TOP

Rotating 270 degrees CLOCKWISE is the same as rotating 90 degrees COUNTER-CLOCKWISE. The notch started on the RIGHT (3 o'clock position). After 270 degrees CW: right goes to top. Option A shows 90 degrees CW (notch at bottom); Option B shows 180 degrees (notch at left); Option D is unchanged; Option E is a diagonal misread.

Q2: C. 24 cubes

Each face of the 4x4x4 cube has a 4x4 grid of unit faces. The unit cubes with EXACTLY ONE face painted sit in the 2x2 interior of each face (not on the edges or corners). So 2 x 2 = 4 such cubes per face, times 6 faces = 24. Option A (8) is the corner count (3 faces painted). Option B (16) confuses 4x4 with 4x2.

Q3: C. A large black pentagon

Rows change shape: triangle, square, pentagon. Columns change size+fill together: small white, medium gray, large black. Row 3, column 3 = large black pentagon. Option A keeps the wrong column values. D changes the shape. E mixes size and fill.

Q4: C. 12 cubes

For a 3x3x3 cube, unit cubes with exactly 2 faces painted sit on the EDGES but not at corners. A cube has 12 edges; each edge has one middle cube (the ones between the two corner cubes on that edge). So 12 edge-middle cubes with 2 faces painted. Corners (8) have 3 faces painted; face centers (6) have 1 face painted; one interior cube (1) has 0 faces painted. Total check: 8+12+6+1 = 27. Correct.

Q5: C. 486

The rule is geometric: next = 3 x current. Check: 2 x 3 = 6, 6 x 3 = 18, 18 x 3 = 54, 54 x 3 = 162, 162 x 3 = 486. Option A (216) is 162 + 54 (arithmetic instead of geometric). Option E (648) is 162 x 4.

Q6: B. Circle

A plane parallel to the cone's circular base produces a smaller CIRCLE. The cone is a cone, and every horizontal slice is a circular cross-section (similar to the base but smaller). Option C (ellipse) would require the slice to be at an angle. Option A (triangle) would come from slicing vertically through the apex. Option D (parabola) would come from an angled slice parallel to one slant side.

Q7: C. 2 holes, mirrored across the fold line

When paper is folded in half (2 layers) and a hole is punched, the hole passes through both layers. When unfolded, the two holes are symmetric across the fold line. A hole in the upper-left of the folded piece produces a matching hole in the upper-right of the unfolded sheet. Option A ignores the layering. Option D assumes 4 folds. Option E mis-positions the holes.

Q8: A. Both squares shaded, overlap rectangle empty

"Union minus intersection" is the symmetric difference: the region inside either square but NOT inside both. Visually, this is both squares with the overlap rectangle left empty, producing two L-shaped shaded regions. Option B shows only the intersection. C shows the full union. D shows only one square. Same set-theory concept as Test 05 Q8 but applied to squares instead of circles.

Q9: B. 7.5 degrees

At 3:15, the minute hand points directly at 3 (90 degrees from 12). The hour hand is NOT at 3 exactly; it has moved 1/4 of the way from 3 to 4 (because 15 minutes have elapsed). Each hour mark is 30 degrees, so the hour hand has advanced 30 x (15/60) = 7.5 degrees past the 3 position. Angle between: 97.5 - 90 = 7.5 degrees. Option A (0) is the common wrong answer for students who think both hands are at 3 exactly.

Q10: B. Rectangle (non-square)

A non-square rectangle has 180 degrees rotational symmetry (half-turn looks identical) but NOT 90 degrees (quarter-turn makes width and height swap, which visibly changes the shape). Option A (square) has BOTH 90 and 180 rotational symmetry. Option C (equilateral triangle) has 120 degrees symmetry, not 180. Option D (L-shape) has no rotational symmetry. Option E (circle) has every angle of rotational symmetry.

Section C: Logic-Based Reasoning

Q21: A. Officer Park holds a valid TS/SCI clearance

A NECESSARY condition means: if X happens (Park works in SOC), then Y must be true (Park has TS/SCI). By modus ponens on the necessary-condition statement: Park works in SOC, so Park must hold TS/SCI. Option B reverses the direction (making TS/SCI sufficient, not just necessary). Option D confuses necessary with sufficient. This is the classic "only if" direction check.

Q22: A. NORTH: T. EAST: P, R, S. SOUTH: Q

Check all three constraints against option A:

(1) P and Q at different posts: P at EAST, Q at SOUTH. OK.

(2) R and S at same post: both at EAST. OK.

(3) T alone at NORTH: T is the only officer at NORTH. OK.

Option B puts P at NORTH with T, violating constraint 3. Option C puts P and Q both at SOUTH, violating constraint 1. Option D puts P at NORTH instead of T, violating constraint 3. Option E puts P and Q both at EAST, violating constraint 1.

Q23: D. 24 assignments

Four distinct officers to four distinct posts, one each, is the number of permutations of 4 items: 4! = 4 x 3 x 2 x 1 = 24. Option A (4) counts officers only. Option C (16) is 4^2, for 2 posts. Option E (256) is 4^4, the number if each officer could independently pick any of 4 posts with repeats.

Q24: A. Exactly one of Owens or Park is lying

Owens says "Park is lying." Park says "Owens is lying." This forms a contradiction pair: O and P have OPPOSITE truth values. If O is truthful, then P lies, and P's statement "O lies" is false, which is consistent. If O lies, then P doesn't lie, and P's statement "O lies" is true, also consistent. Either way, EXACTLY ONE of O, P is lying. Nelson and Quinn form a mutual-affirmation pair, so they have the SAME truth value (either both truthful or both lying). Option C (both N and Q truthful) is only sometimes true. Option B is impossible because N and Q cannot have different truth values. Option D and E are impossible because at least one must be lying (the O/P contradiction forces at least one lie).

Q25: A. Officer Lane is certified for carry duty

This is classic modus ponens: "If P then Q. P is true. Therefore Q." Lane completed the course (P), so Lane is certified (Q). Option B contradicts the conclusion. Option D confuses "if then" with "only if." Option E is too cautious; modus ponens gives a definite answer.

Q26: B. The response team did NOT acknowledge

Rule: (sensor fires AND ack) -> log. Given NOT log and sensor fired. Modus tollens on the conjunction: NOT log -> NOT (sensor AND ack) = NOT sensor OR NOT ack. Given sensor fired (so NOT sensor is false), only NOT ack can be true. Therefore the response team did NOT acknowledge. Option A contradicts the derivation. Option C contradicts a given fact.

Q27: D. 96 inquiries

Expected value: 12 inquiries/hour x 8 hours = 96 inquiries. This is the simplest form of expected frequency: multiply the rate by the duration. Option B (48) uses a 4-hour shift. Option E (120) uses 10 hours.

Q28: D. It cannot be determined

Premises: K9 handlers are all UD (subset). Some UD serve on CS team. We know K9 handlers are a subset of UD, and SOME UD are on CS, but we do not know whether any K9 handlers fall within that "some." The K9 handlers might all be non-CS, or some might be CS; the premises do not constrain this. Option B ("some K9 are CS") is a common quantifier trap (the "undistributed middle"). Options A and C over-state the premises.

Q29: E. Saturday

Day 1 is Monday. The day of the week follows a 7-day cycle. Day 20 = Day 1 + 19 days. 19 mod 7 = 5. Monday + 5 = Saturday. The 4-on-2-off schedule is a distraction; the question asks only for the day of the week, which depends on the 7-day cycle, not the duty cycle.

Q30: B. S1 = T, S2 = T, S3 = F, S4 = T

Verify:

S1 = T claims S2 = T. S2 is T. Consistent.

S2 = T claims S1 = T. S1 is T. Consistent.

S3 = F claims "exactly two of four are true." Actual count: S1, S2, S4 are true (3 true). The claim "exactly 2 true" is FALSE (actual is 3), so S3 being F matches.

S4 = T claims S1 is true. S1 is T. Consistent.

All four statements align under (T, T, F, T). Option A (T,T,T,F) fails: S3=T claims "exactly 2 true" but actual count = 3 (S1, S2, S3), so statement false, S3=T contradicts. Option C (F,F,F,T) fails: S4=T claims S1 is true, but S1=F, contradiction. Option D (F,F,T,F) fails: S3=T claims "exactly 2 true" but only S3 itself is true (count = 1), so statement false, S3=T contradicts.

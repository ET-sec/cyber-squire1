Section A — Figural Reasoning

1. A — 180° rotation flips top-bottom AND left-right. The F lands upside-down and backward. Option A shows that exact state.

2. D — 14 total squares: 9 small (3×3), 4 medium (2×2 overlapping), 1 large (whole grid). 9 + 4 + 1 = 14.

3. B — Columns keep shape (square/circle/triangle), rows add internal lines (empty → 1 bar → X). Row 3 × column 3 = triangle with X inside.

4. C — Front rectangle, top circle, side rectangle — a cylinder lying on its side (circle from above, rectangles from front/side).

5. C — Differences: +4, +6, +8, so next is +10 = 30. (Second differences = 2, so the sequence is n²+n: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30.)

6. E — A–D are all rotations of the same L-tetromino (4 squares in an L shape). E is a T-tetromino — a different piece that cannot be obtained by rotating the L.

7. D — Each fold doubles the layer count: 1 → 2 → 4 → 8 layers. One punch through 8 layers creates 8 holes when unfolded.

8. A — The overlap region is a lens-shaped area where the circle and square intersect. Option A depicts just that region.

9. A — Outer rotates 45° CW each step: up → up-right → right → DOWN-RIGHT. Inner rotates 45° CCW: up → up-left → left → DOWN-LEFT. Next figure: outer down-right, inner down-left.

10. A — Shapes: triangle(3) → square(4) → pentagon(5) → hexagon(6) → heptagon(7). Dots cycle 1, 2, 3, 1, 2. So heptagon with 2 dots.

Section C — Logic-Based Reasoning

21. B — Chain modus tollens. Scene→DNA→99%. Match is 47% (NOT >99%), so NOT DNA, so NOT at scene.

22. A — Exactly 3 of 5 are bilingual. Diaz is (1/3 known). Cole and Evers are NOT (2 of the 2 known non-bilingual). The remaining 2 slots for bilingual must be Azar AND Bray. Both are bilingual.

23. B — "Alarm → dispatch within 30 seconds." Dispatch called at 14:02:15 means alarm was no earlier than 14:02:15 minus 30 seconds = 14:01:45. Could be later. Answer: no earlier than 14:01:45.

24. A — "Only vehicles with red placards may park in VIP." Vehicle X IS parked there, so it has a red placard (modus ponens via "only if" = necessary).

25. B — D is 4th. C→F must be IMMEDIATELY consecutive. Working through the options: only B (A, E, B, D, C, F) satisfies all five constraints — A(1) before C(5), C(5)→F(6) consecutive, D at position 4, B(3) after E(2), and E not first.

26. A — "Requires Level 2 → requires witness signature." No witness signature. Modus tollens: NOT Level 2 required. A only. (B and D claim more than the rule states.)

27. B — "No Lee-handled evidence has been disputed." Bag 1199 has been disputed, so Lee did not handle it.

28. C — Biconditional: unlock ↔ (biometric AND keycard). NOT unlock ↔ NOT (biometric AND keycard) ↔ (NOT biometric OR NOT keycard). At least one failed. (D claims BOTH failed — too strong; only one failure is needed.)

29. C — "Overnight → per-diem" does NOT mean "per-diem → overnight" (affirming the consequent trap). Per-diem may be given for other reasons. Cannot determine.

30. B — Test each case. If Alvarez is truthful: "Bates is lying" is true → Bates's claim "Cruz is lying" is false → Cruz is truthful. But then 2 people truthful, contradicts "exactly one." If Bates is truthful: "Cruz is lying" true → Cruz's claim "both lying" is false → at least one of Alvarez/Bates is telling truth (Bates is — consistent). Alvarez's claim "Bates is lying" would be false → Alvarez lying. So Alvarez liar, Cruz liar, Bates truthful. Exactly one truthful ✓. If Cruz is truthful: "Both lying" true → Alvarez lying ("Bates is lying" is false, so Bates truthful) — contradicts Cruz being the only truthful one. So only Bates works.

Scoring (Test 03). 27–30: you're ready. 22–26: drill the harder LBR patterns (biconditionals, exactly-N quantifiers, truth-teller puzzles). 17–21: spend a week on contrapositive drills. ≤16: return to Test 01.

Why Test 03 is harder than 01–02:
1. Back-to-back observation creates memory INTERFERENCE — details from Scene 6 actively compete with Scene 5 recall. Top candidates rehearse Scene 5 details during Scene 6 study.
2. LBR introduces BICONDITIONALS (Q28), EXACTLY-N quantifiers (Q22), and TRUTH-TELLER puzzles (Q30) — patterns that appear on the actual SAEE's hardest LBR passages.
3. Figural Q7 (three folds, 8 layers) and Q9 (counter-rotating arrows) require simultaneous tracking of two rules — a known skill ceiling on the real exam.

Section A — Figural Reasoning (Q1–10)

1. B — Up → up-right (45° CW) → right (90°) → down-right (135°) → next is straight DOWN (180°).

2. B — 6 triangles total. Only triangles bounded by drawn segments: (1) outer whole triangle; (2) left half; (3) right half; (4) top half above the horizontal line; (5) top-left small; (6) top-right small. The bottom trapezoids are NOT triangles — no drawn segment connects the horizontal line's endpoints to the base midpoint.

3. E — Each row goes: empty → one line → two crossed lines. Row 3 triangle with X inside.

4. D — Two folds create 4 layers. One punch at a corner of the folded piece creates 4 holes when fully unfolded, one in each corner.

5. C — C preserves the object's identity (same proportions, dot position moves to the adjacent face as rotation predicts). A is a mirror. D and E are entirely different shapes.

6. B — Differences are +2 each step: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9. Next is 9 dots.

7. D — Assume a regular pentagon. A regular pentagon has 5 lines of symmetry — one through each vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side.

8. A — The rule from A→B is: rotate 90° clockwise AND add an inner dot. Apply to C: rotate the T 90° CW, then add a dot.

9. B — Fold the net using square 3 as the bottom face. Square 1 folds to the back, square 6 folds to the front, so 1 & 6 are opposite. Squares 2 and 4 become left and right (opposite). Square 5 wraps over 4 to become the TOP. So 2 & 5 are left-and-top — ADJACENT, not opposite. Only 1 & 6 is an opposite pair.

10. B — Each step: +1 side, −5 radius. Triangle (3, r30) → Square (4, r25) → Pentagon (5, r20) → Hexagon (6, r15) → Heptagon (7, r10).

Section A — Figural Reasoning (Q1–10)

1. A — 180° rotation flips top-bottom AND left-right. The F lands upside-down and backward.

2. D — 14 total squares: 9 small (3×3), 4 medium (2×2 overlapping), 1 large (whole grid). 9 + 4 + 1 = 14.

3. B — Columns keep shape (square/circle/triangle), rows add internal lines (empty → 1 bar → X). Row 3 × column 3 = triangle with X inside.

4. C — Front rectangle, top circle, side rectangle — a cylinder lying on its side.

5. C — Differences: +4, +6, +8, so next is +10 = 30. (Sequence is n²+n: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30.)

7. D — Each fold doubles layer count: 1 → 2 → 4 → 8 layers. One punch through 8 layers creates 8 holes when unfolded.

8. A — The overlap region is a lens-shaped area where the circle and square intersect.

10. A — Shapes: triangle(3) → square(4) → pentagon(5) → hexagon(6) → heptagon(7). Dots cycle 1, 2, 3, 1, 2. So heptagon with 2 dots.

Scoring guide (per test, out of 30).
27–30: exam-ready.
22–26: solid — drill the weak section.
17–21: study LBR contrapositives, cube nets, and observation chunking.
≤16: slow down and rebuild fundamentals before attempting the next test.

Across all three tests (out of 90): 80+ is strong; 70–79 is a passing zone with room to tighten; below 70 needs targeted drill before the real SAEE.

LBR traps to memorize: Affirming the consequent (P→Q and Q does NOT give you P). Denying the antecedent (P→Q and not-P does NOT give you not-Q). Valid moves are modus ponens and modus tollens only.

Observation tips: Plates and numbers are the most-tested details on the real exam — read each one twice, mentally chunk (GTV-419 = "letters then numbers"). Count people deliberately, including uniformed staff and children. Note clothing color, distinctive items, and direction of movement.

Cube nets: Pick one square as the "bottom" and mentally fold each adjacent square up. Squares that start three positions apart along a straight strip end up opposite; squares two apart end up adjacent.

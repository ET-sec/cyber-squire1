SECTION C — Logic-Based Reasoning (21–30)

Harder set. Treat all premises as TRUE. Use ONLY the information given.

21. If Agent Torres is assigned to the motorcade, then Agent Yu is assigned to the site advance. If Agent Yu is assigned to the site advance, then Agent Kim is assigned to the command post. Agent Kim is NOT assigned to the command post. Which conclusion follows?

  • A. Agent Yu is assigned to the site advance.
  • B. Agent Torres is assigned to the motorcade.
  • C. Agent Torres is NOT assigned to the motorcade.
  • D. Agent Yu is NOT assigned to the site advance, but Torres could still be on the motorcade.
  • E. No conclusion can be drawn about any of the agents.

22. Every piece of evidence logged into the vault by 17:00 is available for the morning review. Some pieces logged AFTER 17:00 are also available for the morning review. Evidence #4411 is available for the morning review. Which must be true?

Section A — Figural Reasoning

1. B — Up → up-right (45° CW) → right (90°) → down-right (135°) → next is straight DOWN (180°).

2. B — 6 triangles total. The only triangles bounded by drawn line segments are: (1) the whole outer triangle; (2) left half (apex, bottom-left, bottom-midpoint); (3) right half (apex, bottom-right, bottom-midpoint); (4) top half above the horizontal line (apex, left-cross, right-cross); (5) top-left small (apex, left-cross, median-cross); (6) top-right small (apex, right-cross, median-cross). The bottom trapezoids are NOT triangles because there is no drawn segment from the horizontal line's left or right endpoint to the base midpoint.

3. E — Each row goes: empty → one line → two crossed lines (X). Row 3 triangle with X inside.

4. D — Two folds create 4 layers. One punch at a corner of the folded piece creates 4 holes when fully unfolded, one in each corner.

5. C — C preserves the object's identity (same proportions, dot position moves to the adjacent face as rotation predicts). A is a mirror — left-right flipped. D and E are entirely different shapes.

6. B — Differences are +2 each step: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9. Next is 9 dots.

7. D — Assume a regular pentagon. A regular pentagon has 5 lines of symmetry — one through each vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side.

8. A — The rule from A→B is: rotate 90° clockwise AND add an inner dot. Apply to C: rotate the T 90° CW, then add a dot.

9. B — Fold the net using square 3 as the bottom face. Square 1 (above 3) folds up to the back, square 6 (below 3) folds up to the front — so 1 & 6 are opposite. Squares 2 and 4 (the two middle squares flanking 3) fold up to become left and right — so 2 & 4 are opposite. Square 5 (far right of the middle row) wraps over 4 to land on the TOP face, opposite 3. Therefore 2 & 5 are NOT opposite (they end up as left-and-top, which are adjacent). Only 1 & 6 is an opposite pair.

10. B — Each step: +1 side, -5 radius. Triangle (3, r30) → Square (4, r25) → Pentagon (5, r20) → Hexagon (6, r15) → Heptagon (7, r10).

Section C — Logic-Based Reasoning

21. C — Chain modus tollens. Torres→Yu, Yu→Kim. NOT Kim, therefore NOT Yu, therefore NOT Torres.

22. C — Logged-by-17:00 implies available, but the reverse is not stated. Available evidence could be from before or after 17:00. Time of logging cannot be determined.

23. A — "No badge → escorted off" by contrapositive means "not escorted off → has a badge." Marcus is not escorted off, so he has a badge. (B is not forced — the rule gives badge to authorized visitors, but having a badge doesn't prove authorization in the direction given.)

24. A — "Reliable ONLY IF (line of sight AND within 50 feet)" means reliable → both conditions. By contrapositive, if NOT line of sight, then NOT reliable. Unreliable.

25. A — R is 5th. Q immediately before T: (…Q,T…). P before Q. S before P. Only order: S, P, Q, T, R.

26. C — The rule says "fewer than 12 points → NOT admissible." It does NOT say "12+ points → admissible." 12+ is necessary but may not be sufficient. Admissibility could depend on other factors.

27. E — Chen responded Tuesday, so Chen is on graveyard shift. No graveyard is on swing, so Chen NOT on swing. Also, all Tuesday responders were graveyard, so no swing shift responded. Both B and D follow.

28. E — Two contrapositives chained. Rule 2: "north closed → east open" contrapositive is "east NOT open → north NOT closed." East is closed, so north is OPEN (confirms D). Rule 1: "west open → north closed" contrapositive is "north NOT closed → west NOT open." North is open, so west is NOT open (confirms B). Both B and D follow — answer is E.

29. C — "Most field agents are trained" does not mean "most trained are field agents." A few non-field agents are also trained. Bell being trained could make him either. Cannot determine.

30. C — The premises contradict: only Level 3 vehicles may enter; 774 entered; 774 is Level 1. At least one premise must be false. This is an internal-inconsistency question — the correct response is recognizing the contradiction, not inventing exceptions (A, B, D, E all invent facts).

Scoring. 27–30: exam-ready. 22–26: solid. 17–21: drill contrapositives + observation chunking. ≤16: restart fundamentals.

LBR pattern library. Chain contrapositives (Q21, Q27, Q28) are the most common advanced trap. "Only if" = necessary condition (Q24, Q30). "If and only if" = both directions (Test 01 Q28). "Most" and "some" never give you certainty about individuals (Q29). Contradiction-detection (Q30) is tested occasionally — the right move is to recognize the premises can't all be true, not to invent a resolution.

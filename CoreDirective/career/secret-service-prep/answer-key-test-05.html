Section A: Figural Reasoning

Q1: A. Arrow pointing up

The original arrow points RIGHT. A 90° counter-clockwise rotation moves the tip from RIGHT to UP. Option B (down) would be 90° clockwise; C (left) would be 180°; D and E are not rotations. Trap: candidates under time pressure often reverse the rotation direction. Memorize "CCW moves a rightward arrow upward."

Q2: D. 30 squares

Count by size on the 4×4 grid: 16 single cells (1×1) + 9 two-by-two squares (3 horizontal starting positions × 3 vertical) + 4 three-by-three + 1 whole grid (4×4). 16 + 9 + 4 + 1 = 30. Option A (16) counts only unit cells; B (20) is a common partial count; E (36) uses the 3×3 formula from Test 03 by mistake.

Q3: B. Pentagon with 3 dots

Two independent rules. Columns: each column holds a fixed shape. Column 1 is triangles, column 2 is squares, column 3 is pentagons. Rows: each row adds one dot. Row 1 has one dot per figure, row 2 has two, row 3 has three. The missing cell is row 3, column 3: pentagon (column 3) with three dots (row 3). Option A keeps the shape right but wrong dot count; D repeats column 2's shape; E repeats column 1's shape.

Q4: A. X front, T top, B bottom, L left, R right, Bk back

The cross-shaped net is the canonical cube unfolding. With X as the front face and the arrow on X pointing up, the five neighboring squares fold cleanly: T above X folds up to the TOP face; B below X folds down to the BOTTOM; L and R on either side become the LEFT and RIGHT faces; Bk at the far end of the row wraps around to the BACK. Option B reverses T with B and L with R. Option C rotates the whole cube (arrow points right instead of up, and labels swap accordingly). Option D turns the cube so Bk is forward, which contradicts the specified orientation. Option E reverses the arrow direction on X.

Q5: D. 95

The rule is nnext = 2×n + 1. Check: 2, 5, 11, 23, 47. Then 2×47 + 1 = 95. Alternative framing: each difference doubles (+3, +6, +12, +24, +48). Trap: adding 24 again instead of 48 yields 71 (option A). 96 (option E) comes from the "double the number then add 2" misread.

Q6: E. The Z-tetromino

Figures A through D are all the S-tetromino in one of its two rotational orientations (an S-piece has only two distinct rotations in the plane, so A=C and B=D). Figure E is the Z-tetromino, the MIRROR image of the S-piece. Reflections are not allowed by rotation alone, so E cannot be produced from the others. Trap: S and Z pieces look so similar that test-takers see five "same" figures.

Q7: E. 16 holes

Each fold in half doubles the layer count: fold 1 = 2 layers, fold 2 = 4, fold 3 = 8, fold 4 = 16. A single punch passes through all 16 layers simultaneously, so the unfolded sheet shows 16 holes. Trap: candidates often count folds as "+1 hole" (yielding 5) or use 2×fold (yielding 8) instead of 2 raised to the fold count.

Q8: A. Both crescents shaded, overlap empty

Symmetric difference = (A union B) minus (A intersect B) = the region inside EXACTLY one circle, not both. Visually, that is the two outer crescents with the center lens left empty. Option B shades only the intersection (the lens), which is the opposite of what the question asks. Option C shades the full union, including the lens. Option D shades only the left crescent, which is half of the correct answer. This question separates students who confuse inclusive OR ("either or both") from exclusive OR ("either but not both").

Q9: A. Outer UP, inner DOWN

Track both rotations independently.

Outer (90° CW per step): Step 0 UP, Step 1 RIGHT, Step 2 DOWN, Step 3 LEFT, Step 4 UP (a full 360°).

Inner (45° CCW per step): Step 0 UP, Step 1 UP-LEFT, Step 2 LEFT, Step 3 DOWN-LEFT, Step 4 DOWN (180° total).

At step 4 the outer has completed exactly one full revolution back to UP; the inner has swept through exactly half a revolution to DOWN. Trap options B, C, and D tempt candidates who lose count mid-rotation; E picks plausible diagonals.

Q10: B. Heptagon, hatched, 5 dots

Three parallel rules must all advance.

Shape (+1 side per step): triangle(3), square(4), pentagon(5), hexagon(6), heptagon(7).

Dots (+1 per step): 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.

Shading (3-cycle: empty, hatched, solid): empty, hatched, solid, empty, hatched (position 5 = position 2 in the cycle).

Heptagon with hatched fill and 5 dots. Option A keeps the right shape but advances shading wrongly; C over-advances shape; D skips shape; E fails shading.

Section B: Observation with Interference

Q11: A. Grand Meridian Hotel

The gold letters on the dark blue sign spelled "GRAND MERIDIAN HOTEL." The other options are close cousins ("Meridian Plaza" and "Grand Sheraton") chosen to catch candidates who retained only part of the name.

Q12: C. 10:42

The analog wall clock in Scene 9 showed hands pointing to roughly 10:42 (hour hand near 11, minute hand near the 8 position), with the label "10:42" directly below. Scene 10's clock showed 08:17. Do not let that later detail overwrite this one.

Q13: C. Dark pantsuit and briefcase

The protectee wore a dark pantsuit (the SVG uses navy/near-black) and carried a dark briefcase on her right. Option A ("red dress with blue sash") is the classic diplomatic-reception red herring; option D describes the woman from Scene 10, a pure interference trap.

Q14: C. Room 402 VIP

A small green-on-black monospace sign near the elevator read "ROOM 402 VIP." The whole number (402) and the tag (VIP) both had to be retained for this answer to lock in. "Presidential Suite" (D) is the generic-sounding distractor.

Q15: B. A camera and a blue cap

Far right, near the revolving door, a figure wore a blue cap and held a dark camera at chest level. This is the detail you would be expected to report on an actual protective detail: an unidentified person photographing the protectee near an exit. Option A (briefcase and gray suit) describes the gray-suited man from Scene 10 in a different context. Options C, D, and E attach plausible companion items to objects that were never present.

Q16: B. Northern District of Georgia

The sign above the gold seal read "NORTHERN DISTRICT OF GEORGIA" in white on dark blue. The seal's inner abbreviation was "USDC-NDGA" (United States District Court, Northern District of Georgia), which double-confirms B. Georgia has three federal districts (Northern, Middle, Southern), so options A, C, D are plausible misreads; option E is a reach.

Q17: B. 08:17

The digital wall clock displayed "08:17" in red LED against black. Scene 9's 10:42 is the interference trap; retain the scene-to-time mapping explicitly.

Q18: B. Red blazer, black skirt, orange tote bag

The woman second in line wore a red blazer with a black skirt, and carried an orange tote bag. Option A flips the color scheme to yellow; option C describes the gray-suited man from the same scene; option D ("black dress, red purse") anchors to the Scene 5 woman from Test 03; option E offers a generic business look that was not present. If you have drilled the earlier tests in the series, your memory of prior scenes becomes its own interference source.

Q19: A. K-9 unit

A working dog (brown/tan coloring with a harness) stood near the doorway on the right side of the checkpoint. No luggage cart, chandelier, or bank counter appeared in this scene; those were Scene 9 elements (luggage cart and chandelier). This question tests whether you can correctly attribute objects to the CORRECT scene under interference.

Q20: C. Both scenes, Scene 9 at 10:42 and Scene 10 at 08:17

Both clocks displayed morning hours (before noon). Scene 9's analog clock at 10:42 and Scene 10's digital clock at 08:17 both fall in AM. Candidates who misread one as PM lock in a partially correct answer (A or B). This is the highest-leverage cross-scene question in the set; it rewards explicit rehearsal of both times during the 90-second study window, not passive viewing.

Section C: Logic-Based Reasoning

Q21: D. At least one clause failed (biometric, keycard, or both authorization codes)

The rule is a BICONDITIONAL with a COMPOUND right side: open ⇔ [(bio ∧ key) ∧ (sup ∨ emer)]. If the vault did not open, by contrapositive the right side is false: ¬[(bio ∧ key) ∧ (sup ∨ emer)] ⇒ ¬(bio ∧ key) ∨ ¬(sup ∨ emer) ⇒ (¬bio ∨ ¬key) ∨ (¬sup ∧ ¬emer). At least one of those three disjuncts is the culprit. Option A adds an unsupported second conjunct (that no supervisor code was entered). Option B picks two specific failures when only one is required. Option C claims both authorization codes failed but says nothing about biometric or keycard. Option E invokes a malfunction not licensed by the premises.

Q22: A. A, D, C, E, F, G, B

Verify each constraint against A, D, C, E, F, G, B:

(1) A immediately before D: positions 1 and 2, consecutive. OK.

(2) F is fifth: position 5 is F. OK.

(3) C before F: C at position 3, F at position 5. OK.

(4) B is last: position 7 is B. OK.

(5) E is not first: E at position 4. OK.

Option B reverses A and D (D before A). Option C puts C between A and D (violates "immediately"). Option D moves C to position 5 and F to position 6. Option E has E at position 1, which violates constraint (5).

Q23: B. 6

Pigeonhole principle: with 32 agents across 6 zones, the best-distributed (lowest maximum) arrangement is 32 = 6+6+5+5+5+5. Some zone must have at least ⌈32/6⌉ = 6 agents. If everyone went into one bucket the maximum could be higher, but the QUESTION asks the minimum forced maximum, which is 6. Option A (5) fails because 6 zones of 5 = 30, leaving 2 unassigned. Trap: students often compute 32/6 = 5.33 and round DOWN.

Q24: E. No pair is consistent

Test all six possible truth-pairs against "exactly two truthful":

Alvarez + Cruz: Alvarez says "Bates is lying," so Bates lies. Cruz says "Alvarez truthful," consistent. But then Dietz's statement "exactly one of A, B is lying" is TRUE (A truthful, B lying = 1 lying), which means Dietz is truthful, yet we said Dietz lies. Contradiction.

Alvarez + Dietz: Alvarez says "B lying," OK. Dietz says "exactly 1 of A, B lying"; with A truthful and B lying, Dietz is true. OK. But Cruz lying means "A truthful" is false, so A lies. Contradiction (A was supposed to be truthful).

Bates + Cruz: Bates says "C AND D truthful," which forces Dietz to be truthful. That gives 3 truthful, not 2. Contradiction.

Bates + Dietz: Bates says "C AND D truthful," which forces Cruz truthful. That gives 3 truthful. Contradiction.

Cruz + Dietz: Cruz says "A truthful," which forces Alvarez truthful. That gives 3 truthful. Contradiction.

Alvarez + Bates: Alvarez says Bates is lying, but Bates is supposed to be truthful. Direct contradiction.

No pair works. This is a designed INCONSISTENT puzzle. On the real exam, candidates waste minutes trying to force a solution. Recognize the pattern and mark E quickly.

Q25: C. The subject was either not armed, or not resistant, or both

The rule is (armed ∧ resistant) → force. Apply modus tollens on NOT force: ¬force → ¬(armed ∧ resistant) = ¬armed ∨ ¬resistant. At least one of the two conditions failed. Option A picks only the armed condition; option B picks only the resistance condition. Both are too strong because the conjunction requires only one missing piece to break. Option D claims BOTH failed, which is also too strong (only one failure is required to void the trigger). Option E assumes a protocol violation not implied by the premises.

Q26: A. Gate A is open

Exclusive OR: exactly one of A, B is open, not both. Given B is closed, the "exactly one open" requirement must be filled by A, so A is open. If A were also closed, zero gates would be open, violating the rule. Trap: students who confuse XOR with inclusive OR may pick D ("cannot determine") because inclusive OR leaves A's state open.

Q27: C. 44%

Conditional probability: P(sidearm | radio) = P(sidearm ∧ radio) / P(radio) = 0.40 / 0.90 = 4/9 ≈ 0.444 = 44.4%. Closest option is 44%. Option B (40%) is the raw joint probability, a common confusion. E (90%) is the radio probability alone.

Q28: D. Cannot be concluded

The premises say K-9 implies L3 implies monthly drills. Tate does monthly drills, but drills may be required of many other roles too. Inferring "Tate is K-9" or "Tate is L3" from "Tate does drills" is AFFIRMING THE CONSEQUENT, the classic logic fallacy the exam tests repeatedly. A, B, and C each commit this fallacy. E ("supervisor") is unrelated. Answer: cannot determine.

Q29: B. Event 4 occurs at 16:30

Fix the anchor points. E1 at 13:00, E5 at 18:00. E3 = E1 + 2:00 = 15:00. E2 between E1 and E3, so 13:00 < E2 < 15:00, which cannot be pinned exactly. E4 and E5 are 90 min apart; because events are numbered consecutively through the day, E4 must precede E5, so E4 = 18:00 − 1:30 = 16:30.

Options A and D guess a specific time for E2 that is not forced. Option C claims E3 = 16:00, which is incorrect; E3 = 15:00. Option E is wrong because the anchors give us E1, E3, E4, and E5 exactly. Only B must be true.

Q30: A. S1 = true, S2 = true, S3 = false

Walk through the assignment:

S3 = false: S3 says "S1 is false." If S3 is false, then "S1 is false" is false, so S1 is TRUE. Consistent.

S1 = true: S1 says "at least one of S2 or S3 is true." S2 is true, so the disjunction holds. Consistent.

S2 = true: S2 says "exactly one of S1 or S3 is true." S1 true, S3 false, so exactly one. Consistent.

All three statements are self-consistent under this assignment. Every other option fails at least one clause (you can test each by substitution in under 90 seconds).

Why Test 05 is designed to feel harder than the real exam: (1) the Q24 truth-teller is designed with NO solution; almost no real UDEE administration does this, but drilling the pattern teaches you to stop fishing for an answer and flag E; (2) Section B now has two scenes of roughly 16 details each with heavy color overlap between scenes, which is a higher interference load than the typical UDEE observation block; (3) Q21 (nested biconditional) and Q29 (5-event timing chain) demand simultaneous tracking of three or more constraints, which is the top end of the real exam's logic ceiling. If you pass Test 05 at 23+, you will pass the real exam with margin.

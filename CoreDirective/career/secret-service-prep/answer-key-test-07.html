Section A: Figural Reasoning

Q1: A. Arrow left, dot on the RIGHT side

Reflection across a vertical axis flips the figure left-to-right. The arrow tip swings from RIGHT to LEFT. The dot, originally on the LEFT side of the arrow, now sits on the RIGHT side. Option B puts the dot on the wrong side. C is unchanged. D rotates instead of reflecting. E places the dot in the middle instead of mirroring its position.

Q2: D. 18 rectangles

For an m-by-n grid, the number of rectangles equals C(m+1, 2) × C(n+1, 2). A 2-row by 3-column grid has 3 horizontal lines and 4 vertical lines, so: C(3,2) × C(4,2) = 3 × 6 = 18. Option A (6) counts only single cells. B (9) and C (12) are partial counts. E (24) overcounts.

Q3: B. Blue arrow pointing DOWN

Rows rotate the arrow: row 1 up (0 degrees), row 2 right (90 CW), row 3 down (180). Columns change color: col 1 red, col 2 green, col 3 blue. Row 3, column 3 therefore shows a BLUE arrow pointing DOWN. Option A keeps the row 1 rotation. C and D keep the wrong color. E takes the row 2 rotation.

Q4: B. 4 space diagonals

A cube has 8 vertices. Each vertex has exactly one vertex that does NOT share a face with it: the vertex diagonally opposite through the cube's interior. Pairing the 8 vertices into opposite pairs gives 8 / 2 = 4 space diagonals. Option C (6) is the number of faces, not the number of space diagonals. E (12) confuses space diagonals with edges.

Q5: C. 34

The sequence is Fibonacci-style: each term equals the sum of the previous two. Check: 3 + 5 = 8, 5 + 8 = 13, 8 + 13 = 21, 13 + 21 = 34. Option A (28) adds 21 + 7. B (30) is 21 + 9. D (42) doubles 21. E (55) takes one term too far (next after 34).

Q6: E. The plus-pentomino

Figures A through D are all T-pentominoes (3 cells horizontal with a 2-cell stem from the middle) in the four possible planar rotations. Figure E is the plus-pentomino (5 cells arranged as a plus sign), a completely different piece that is NOT a rotation of the T. The plus-pentomino has 4-fold symmetry (looks the same from every 90 degree rotation), while the T has only 1-fold symmetry.

Q7: D. 8 holes

Two folds in half create 2^2 = 4 layers. Each punched hole passes through all 4 layers, creating 4 holes on the unfolded sheet. Two separate holes therefore create 2 × 4 = 8 total holes. Option A ignores the layering. B counts only one punch's worth. E (16) over-counts by doubling incorrectly.

Q8: A. Three pairwise lenses shaded, center triple region empty

"Inside exactly two of the three circles" means the three pairwise overlap lenses MINUS the central triple overlap. The shaded region forms three crescent-like slivers that meet at, but do not include, the center triangular triple-overlap. Option B shades only the center (inside all three). Option C shades the full union. Option D shades the outer single-circle moons (inside exactly one). None of those match "exactly two."

Q9: C. Southeast

Rotating 45 degrees COUNTER-CLOCKWISE from North cycles: N to NW to W to SW to S to SE to E to NE to N. Five steps from N: step 1 NW, step 2 W, step 3 SW, step 4 S, step 5 SE. Formally: (5 × 45) = 225 degrees CCW from N = SE. Trap answers A (NE) and E (W) are different step counts from the same rule.

Q10: B. Larger than step 4, pointing DOWN

Two simultaneous rules: the arrow grows each step, and the direction rotates 45 degrees clockwise each step. Step 1 UP, step 2 NE (45 CW), step 3 E (90 CW), step 4 SE (135 CW), step 5 S = DOWN (180 CW). Size continues to grow, so step 5 is LARGER than step 4. Option A keeps the same size. C advances direction wrongly. D shrinks. E fails both rules.

Section C: Logic-Based Reasoning

Q21: C. The incident occurred in Zone A or Zone B (or both)

"ONLY X alerts Dispatch" means alert → X. Given that an alert WAS raised, by modus ponens the incident must have been of type X, which is "Zone A or Zone B." We cannot determine which specific zone, so A and B each over-specify. Option D contradicts the rule. Option E is too cautious; the "only if" relationship is strong enough to conclude zone membership.

Q22: A. T, R, P, S, Q, V, U

Verify all six constraints:

(1) P at position 3: yes.

(2) Q immediately after S: S at 4, Q at 5.

(3) T before P: T at 1, before P at 3.

(4) U at position 7: yes.

(5) R not at position 1: R at 2.

(6) V at position 6: yes.

Option B puts P at position 1. Option C puts P at position 5. Option D puts R at position 1. Option E puts Q before S.

Q23: C. 8

Pigeonhole: ⌈37/5⌉ = ⌈7.4⌉ = 8. Distribution of 8+8+7+7+7 = 37 achieves max = 8. Option A (6) fails because 5 × 6 = 30, short by 7 cases. B (7) fails because 5 × 7 = 35, short by 2. Only 8 forces the total to reach 37.

Q24: B. Garcia

Test the three possibilities under "exactly ONE truthful":

Franklin truthful (Garcia and Hayes lying): Franklin says Garcia is lying, consistent. Garcia lying: "Hayes is lying" is false, so Hayes is truthful. But we said Hayes is lying. Contradiction.

Garcia truthful (Franklin and Hayes lying): Garcia says Hayes is lying, consistent. Franklin lying: "Garcia is lying" is false, so Garcia is truthful, consistent. Hayes lying: "Both Franklin and Garcia are lying" is false, so at least one of them is truthful. Garcia is truthful. All checks pass.

Hayes truthful (Franklin and Garcia lying): Hayes says both Franklin and Garcia are lying, consistent. Franklin lying: "Garcia is lying" is false, so Garcia is truthful. But we said Garcia is lying. Contradiction.

Only Garcia works as the lone truthful witness.

Q25: B. The system is NOT functioning normally

The rule is normal → no_alert. An alert WAS raised means no_alert is FALSE. By modus tollens: if the consequent is false, the antecedent must be false. Therefore normal is false; the system is NOT functioning normally. Option A commits affirming the consequent in reverse. Options C and D introduce facts not in the premise. Option E is too weak; modus tollens gives a definitive answer.

Q26: B. The motion sensor was NOT triggered

The rule is (sensor ∧ unlocked) → alarm. Given NOT alarm, by modus tollens: ¬(sensor ∧ unlocked) = ¬sensor ∨ ¬unlocked. At least one of the two conjuncts is false. Given that the door WAS unlocked (so ¬unlocked is false), the only remaining way to satisfy the disjunction is ¬sensor being true: the motion sensor was NOT triggered. Option A contradicts the derivation. C contradicts a given fact. D and E introduce failure modes not supported by the rule.

Q27: D. 75%

Apply Bayes' theorem. Let O = overseas, A = Arabic speaker.

P(O) = 0.60, P(domestic) = 0.40.

P(A|O) = 0.30, P(A|domestic) = 0.15.

Total: P(A) = 0.30(0.60) + 0.15(0.40) = 0.18 + 0.06 = 0.24.

Therefore P(O|A) = P(A|O) × P(O) / P(A) = 0.18 / 0.24 = 0.75 = 75%.

Option A (18%) is P(A ∧ O). Option B (30%) is P(A|O). Option C (60%) is the base rate. Option E (90%) has no basis in the numbers. Only D applies Bayes correctly.

Q28: D. Nothing about Agent Woods's radio can be concluded

The rule is L5 → radio. Given NOT L5. Attempting to conclude NOT radio is DENYING THE ANTECEDENT, a classic logic fallacy. Woods may still carry a radio for other reasons (personal equipment, Level 3 assignment, department issue). The rule states only that L5 is SUFFICIENT for carrying a radio, not that it is NECESSARY. Options A and B each commit this fallacy. Option E claims exclusivity ("only L5") not in the premises.

Note: Test 05 Q28 was an affirming-the-consequent trap. Test 06 Q28 was a valid forward syllogism. Test 07 Q28 is denying-the-antecedent. Three different invalid/valid patterns across the series.

Q29: D. 14:00

Anchor the knowns:

A = 10:00 (given).

B = A + 2:00 = 12:00.

D = 15:00 (given).

C = D − 1:00 = 14:00.

Verify C after B: 14:00 > 12:00. Consistent.

Option E (cannot determine) is the giveaway trap; the four anchors fully determine C's time.

Q30: B. S1 = true, S2 = false, S3 = true

Test the assignment:

S3 = T claims "S1 and S2 cannot both be true" = ¬(S1 ∧ S2) = ¬(T ∧ F) = ¬F = T. Consistent.

S2 = F claims "S1 is false." S1 is T, so the statement is false. A false statement is consistent with S2 being false.

S1 = T claims "S3 is true." S3 is T. Consistent.

All three statements align under (T, F, T).

Test the other options:

A (T, T, T): S2 claims S1 false, but S1 = T. Contradiction.

C (F, T, T): S1 = F claims S3 true; S3 = T makes the claim true, but S1 false requires its statement false. Contradiction.

D (F, F, F): S3 = F claims "S1 and S2 cannot both be true"; the statement is actually true (neither is true), so S3 should be T. Contradiction.

Only B is consistent.

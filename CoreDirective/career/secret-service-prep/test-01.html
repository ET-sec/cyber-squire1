USSS Special Agent Entrance Exam — Practice Test 01

30 questions · Figural Reasoning · Detail Observation · Logic-Based Reasoning · Aligned to 2026 SAEE format
Instructions. Treat this as a timed exam. Suggested pacing:
  • Section A (Figural): 10 questions in 12 minutes.
  • Section B (Observation): Study each scene for 90 seconds. Then answer with the scene covered. 5 questions per scene.
  • Section C (Logic-Based Reasoning): 10 questions in 25 minutes. Treat all premises as TRUE even if absurd. Use ONLY the information given.
Answer key and explanations at the bottom. Do not reveal them until you finish.

SECTION C — Logic-Based Reasoning (21–30)

Treat all premises as TRUE, even if they contradict the real world. Use ONLY the information given. Do not assume what is not stated.

21. All field agents must complete weapons qualification quarterly. Anyone who has not completed weapons qualification this quarter is restricted to administrative duty. Agent Reyes was on protective detail last week. Which conclusion is most strongly supported?

  • A. Reyes is a field agent.
  • B. Reyes completed weapons qualification this quarter.
  • C. Reyes is restricted to administrative duty.
  • D. Protective detail does not require weapons qualification.
  • E. Reyes is a supervisor.

22. If a vehicle enters the secure perimeter without a valid credential, the gate alarm activates. The gate alarm did NOT activate at 14:07. From this alone, what can be concluded?

  • A. No vehicle entered at 14:07.
  • B. Any vehicle that entered at 14:07 had a valid credential.
  • C. The alarm system was disabled.
  • D. Every vehicle in the perimeter has a valid credential.
  • E. A vehicle entered without a credential, but the alarm failed.

23. Every counterfeit bill seized in this district has been printed on bond paper. The bill recovered at the scene was printed on cotton paper. Which statement must be true?

  • A. The bill is genuine.
  • B. The bill was not seized in this district.
  • C. The bill is a counterfeit not previously seized in this district.
  • D. Some counterfeit bills are printed on cotton paper.
  • E. The bill is from a foreign printing operation.

24. No member of the advance team is permitted to carry personal electronics into the secure venue. Officer Hale carried a personal smartwatch into the venue. Which conclusion follows?

  • C. The smartwatch was confiscated.
  • D. Officer Hale is a member of the advance team but used an exemption.

Section A — Figural Reasoning

1. A — The pattern alternates: upright square, rotated square (diamond), upright square, rotated, then upright again. Position 5 returns to upright square.

2. D — All other figures have the dot INSIDE the shape. Only the hexagon has the dot outside.

3. B — A 90° clockwise rotation moves the top-left dot to the top-right corner, and the diagonal goes from top-left to bottom-right. B matches.

4. A — A horizontal mirror flips left/right. The right-angle stays at the bottom but moves to the left side; the dot moves with it.

5. C — Each row holds 1, 2, then 3 dots; row 3 is triangles, so the missing cell is a triangle with 3 dots.

6. E — On a cross-shaped net, the star is the top flap that folds down. The face directly across the cube from it is the bottom flap below the center — the filled dot (●).

7. C — A single hole punched in a folded sheet creates a mirrored pair when unfolded. One hole on the left side of the fold becomes two holes symmetrical across the fold.

8. B — Only B contains the target triangle in the same orientation (apex at top, base at bottom), at roughly the same proportions, embedded inside the larger figure.

9. D — Top → Right → Bottom → Left. The shaded quadrant rotates 90° clockwise each step.

10. B — A is to B as a small shape becomes a small shape concentric inside a larger same-type shape. Small square → small square inside a larger square.

Section C — Logic-Based Reasoning

21. B — If Reyes was on protective detail (a non-administrative assignment), then by contrapositive she must have completed weapons qualification this quarter. A is plausible but not strictly forced — the question only asks what is SUPPORTED, and B follows by direct contrapositive of the second rule.

22. B — By contrapositive: if no alarm sounded, then either no vehicle entered OR every vehicle that entered had a credential. B captures the conditional half. (A is too strong — vehicles WITH credentials could still have entered.)

23. B — All counterfeit bills seized in this district are on bond paper. This bill is on cotton paper, so it cannot be among the counterfeits seized in this district. It might be counterfeit (just not seized here) or genuine — both possible — but the only forced conclusion is that it was not seized in this district.

24. A — Contrapositive: if a person carried personal electronics into the venue, they are NOT on the advance team. The premise tells us nothing about violations or confiscation, so A is the only justified conclusion.

25. B — Diaz is last (4th). Chen relieves Avery directly, so Chen comes immediately after Avery. Avery is before Brown. The only sequence satisfying all three: Avery, Chen, Brown, Diaz.

26. A — Contrapositive: if a statement is admitted, then it was NOT made before counsel arrived. So counsel had arrived first. (B about waiver is plausible in real life but is NOT entailed by the rule given.)

27. A — Contrapositive of "all who passed are bilingual" is "anyone not bilingual did not pass." Park is not bilingual, so Park did not pass. (D is too strong — "did not pass" includes "did not take" the exam.)

28. E — Impoundment requires BOTH conditions (stolen AND on federal property). Knowing only that it's stolen tells you nothing about location. C is tempting but rephrases the rule rather than answering what can be concluded — the prompt asks what we can conclude from the report alone.

29. C — "All on Detail K hold TS clearance" does NOT mean "all TS holders are on Detail K." Vega holding TS clearance is consistent with being on Detail K or not. Cannot determine.

30. A — Contrapositive: if a motorcade was NOT rerouted (used the primary corridor), then the threat level was NOT RED. (D names a specific level — too strong; could have been any non-RED level.)

Scoring guide. 27–30: exam-ready. 22–26: solid, drill weak section. 17–21: study LBR contrapositives and rotation rules. ≤16: rebuild fundamentals before the next test.

Common LBR traps to memorize: Affirming the consequent (if P→Q and Q, you cannot conclude P). Denying the antecedent (if P→Q and ¬P, you cannot conclude ¬Q). The only valid moves are modus ponens (P→Q, P, therefore Q) and modus tollens (P→Q, ¬Q, therefore ¬P).

Observation tips: On the real exam, plates and numbers are the most-tested details — read each one twice, mentally chunk them (GTV-419 = "letters then numbers"). Count people deliberately, including uniformed staff and children. Note clothing color, distinctive items, and direction of movement.

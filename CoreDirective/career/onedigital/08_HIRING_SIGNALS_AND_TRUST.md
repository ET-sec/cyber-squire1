# OneDigital — Hiring Signals, Trust, and Pavel's Decision Framework

What to listen for. What to avoid. How to build trust with a CISA-trained interviewer in 30 minutes.

---

## Pavel's Decision Framework (likely)

A CISA holder deciding on a senior IC hire weights these, roughly in this order:

1. **Control framework fluency** — does the candidate speak in controls, not tools
2. **Evidence + documentation discipline** — does every answer have an auditable trail
3. **Third-party / vendor risk instinct** — does the candidate think about supply chain
4. **Communication clarity** — can they translate for executives AND engineers
5. **AI-specific depth** — can they articulate threat models and governance for AI workloads
6. **Operational judgment** — can they make the right tradeoff under ambiguity
7. **Cultural fit** — do they pattern-match to how the OneDigital security team works
8. **Certifications + credentials** — CISSP in progress, SecurityX, SSCP, CCNA — this is table stakes
9. **Tool-specific experience** — lowest weight, easiest to close

**Implication:** Lead with the top 4 in every answer. Treat the bottom 4 as context, not selling points.

## 10 Positive Hiring Signals (watch for these)

If you see 3+ during the call, you're tracking well.

1. **He asks for specifics.** "Walk me through how you tuned Falco" — deep probe means interest.
2. **He brings up a specific tool.** Asking about Snyk versus Semgrep, or Salt integration with CrowdStrike — means he's modeling you in the role.
3. **He mentions timeline.** "We're looking to move fast" or "panel round would be next week" — scheduling intent.
4. **He references team members.** "You'd work closely with [name] on that" — projecting you into the structure.
5. **He asks about compensation expectations.** Only after he's decided you're viable. Handle carefully (see below).
6. **He volunteers his own concern.** "My biggest worry on this role is..." — inviting you to address it.
7. **He asks when you could start.** Closing-stage question.
8. **He extends the call past 30 minutes without rushing.** Interest buying attention.
9. **He says "next step" or "follow-up" language.** Process moving forward.
10. **He says something like "you've covered that" or "that's good."** Acknowledgment of fit.

---

## 10 Negative Signals (prepare recovery)

1. **He cuts you off.** Stop, acknowledge, ask him to redirect.
2. **He pauses silently after your answer.** You may have missed. Offer a tighter recap.
3. **He doesn't ask follow-up questions.** Not engaging with your content — ask him a clarifier to re-engage.
4. **He sticks rigidly to the JD.** Signals low flexibility, may not see you beyond the tool list.
5. **He asks about salary early.** May be screening out, not in. Anchor HIGH on your end.
6. **He asks about "other candidates."** You're being compared.
7. **He's checking his phone / other monitor.** Distracted; pull him back with a direct question.
8. **He pushes hard on a specific tool gap.** May be a dealbreaker. Deploy the full reframe template, do not hedge.
9. **He mentions "we need someone who can start immediately."** Urgency signal — could be positive, could be negative. Clarify timeline.
10. **He closes without "next step" language.** Send the follow-up email with an explicit nudge ("happy to chat again or do a technical deep-dive on [topic]").

## Trust Killers (words and patterns to avoid)

- "I'll figure it out" — sounds unstructured to a CISA
- "That's not really my thing" — immediate dismissal
- "My last company was..." — focus forward, not backward
- "I don't know" as a full sentence — always follow with "Let me think aloud..." or "I'd approach it by..."
- Over-qualifying ("I'm not an expert, but...") — just answer
- Under-qualifying false confidence ("Oh easy, I've done that tons of times") — specifically with tools you haven't
- "Just" as in "it's just X" — minimizes your own work
- "Basically" used every third sentence — verbal tic
- "Obviously" — makes things sound condescending
- Speaking negatively about a past employer or manager
- Naming names of former coworkers in negative contexts
- "Fake it till you make it" references

## The Post-Salesloft Register (use if it comes up)

You know about the Salesloft / Drift breach. Pavel lived through it. Two rules:

1. **Never lead with it.** Do not bring it up unprompted.
2. **If he brings it up, match his register.** If he's matter-of-fact, be matter-of-fact. If he's frustrated, be empathetic without performing.

**Do not say:**
- "Oh yeah I saw that on the news" — condescending
- "That must have been stressful" — hollow
- "I would have prevented that" — arrogant and implausible
- Specific numbers ("28,414 people") unless he says them first — signals you're reciting rather than understanding

**Do say:**
- "That's a really hard category of risk" (if he signals emotional weight)
- "The remediation playbook is public — what would have been harder in your environment specifically?" (turns it into a forward-looking collaboration)
- "I'd apply the industry playbook and contribute to the next generation of controls — not claim to have prevented what no one prevented"

## The 60-Second Centering Ritual (execute 1 minute before the meeting)

1. Water. Sip slowly.
2. Stand up, shake out your shoulders.
3. Say three times aloud:
   - "I'm an AI Security Engineer."
   - "I've built this end-to-end."
   - "I'm here because the role matches my work."
4. Sit. Camera on. Notepad open. Teams meeting joined.
5. When Pavel appears on screen, smile before you speak.

That's it. You've practiced everything else. The ritual is to arrive calm, not to re-rehearse.

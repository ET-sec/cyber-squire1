# OneDigital — Questions to Ask Pavel

Pick 3-4 from this list based on how the conversation flows. The goal is: signal you think beyond the JD, surface real information, give Pavel something to say that showcases his priorities.

**Delivery rule:** never ask a question you could have Googled. All of these require a human operator to answer.

---

## Tier 1 — Ask These First (high signal, Pavel loves them)

### Q1: "How mature is the AI governance program today, and where is the biggest gap you want closed this year?"

**Why it works:** Acknowledges you understand AI governance is a journey, not a checklist. Invites him to describe his actual pain point, not a polished org summary. His answer tells you whether the role is building-from-zero, refining-existing, or catch-up-against-risk.

**What to listen for:**
- "We have a policy draft but haven't rolled it out" → building
- "We have the policy, need enforcement mechanisms" → refining / implementation
- "We're responding to the Drift incident and trying to get ahead of it" → catch-up / urgency mode

### Q2: "Where does this role sit between security engineering and security operations day-to-day? More build or more run?"

**Why it works:** Forces him to be concrete. Shows you understand those are different disciplines. His answer tells you what the first 90 days look like — will you be coding integrations, writing policies, tuning detection, or all three with weighting shifting by week.

### Q3: "What does the evidence and audit cadence look like for AI-related controls right now? Are you building toward SOC 2 Type 2 on the AI side, ISO 42001, something else?"

**Why it works:** This is a CISA question asked by a non-CISA candidate. Signals you understand the audit lifecycle. Gives him a chance to talk about frameworks he cares about.

### Q4: "What's keeping you up at night on the security side right now?"

**Why it works:** Direct, personal, invites candid answer. Most candidates won't ask it. His answer reveals the actual priority, which may not be what's written in the JD.

## Tier 2 — Ask If the First Ones Opened Good Threads

### Q5: "How is the security team structured, and how does this role interface with the broader IT org?"

Useful for understanding peer relationships, escalation paths, and whether you'd be operating inside a dedicated security team or as a federated specialist inside IT.

### Q6: "What does success look like at the one-year mark for someone in this role?"

Gets concrete about expectations. You want to know whether they'll measure vulnerabilities closed, policies shipped, tools deployed, incidents handled, or something else. Different answers mean different day-to-day behavior.

### Q7: "What tooling changes are you planning in the next 12 months — staying with the current stack or evaluating alternatives?"

OneDigital uses Snyk, Salt, CrowdStrike AIDR, Qualys. Worth knowing if any are under review. If he says "we're committed to the stack," you know the role is optimization. If he says "we're evaluating X," the role includes migration work.

### Q8: "What's the relationship between this role and the acquisition integration work? As OneDigital acquires new firms, who owns the security due diligence and integration?"

Signals you've read about the roll-up model. His answer tells you whether M&A security is part of the job, a separate team, or a shared responsibility.

---

## Tier 3 — Nice to Have (deeper signal, ask only if time permits)

### Q9: "Who are the other security engineers or architects I'd work most closely with? What do their strengths look like?"

Helps you model the team composition. If Pavel names two people and describes complementary strengths, you know the team is mature. If he pauses, you know the team is small.

### Q10: "What's your approach to developer enablement versus gatekeeping? How does security land in the engineering orgs OneDigital has inherited through acquisitions?"

Reveals the culture of the security function. "Enablement first" vs "gatekeeping" tells you a lot about how conflicts get resolved.

### Q11: "ISACA Atlanta Chapter is active — do you engage there? I've been active in OWASP Atlanta and was planning to start ISACA membership this year."

**Only if rapport opening is clear.** Converts a passive question into a professional-community rapport moment. Gives Pavel a chance to be generous with advice about the chapter.

### Q12: "If I picked up the AI Governance policy to read tomorrow, what's the one section I should read closely before we talk again?"

**Reserve this for the close if you're confident a next round is coming.** Signals you want to come prepared, puts a book-end on the interview, gives him something tangible to send you.

---

## Questions to NEVER Ask in the HM Screen

- Anything about salary, bonus, equity, benefits, PTO, vacation, or hybrid flexibility specifics. (Save for post-offer.)
- "What's the company's biggest challenge?" — too vague, doesn't differentiate you
- "What's the culture like?" — Glassdoor answers this
- "Do you offer mentorship?" — either he does or he doesn't, and the answer doesn't inform your decision
- Anything that can be answered by reading OneDigital's career page
- Anything that requires Pavel to speak poorly of a colleague or competitor

---

## The Close

If you have time for one more question after Pavel answers yours:

**"Is there anything in my background you want me to go deeper on before we wrap?"**

This is an invitation for him to probe a concern. If he says "no, we're good" — you likely cleared. If he hesitates, he's going to raise something, which is your last chance to address it inside the interview rather than after.

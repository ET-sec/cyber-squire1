## Section 1: The First 60 Seconds

The first minute decides whether she leans in or leans out. She is on her sixth call of the day. Wake her up.

**Before the call (T-10 minutes):**
- Phone on Do Not Disturb. Slack closed. Telegram muted.
- Water in reach. Resume (AI Security Engineer variant) open on second screen.
- This playbook open on second screen, scrolled to Section 3.
- One-page Dropzone cheat sheet visible (product, founders, funding, customer quotes).
- Stand up. Loosen the jaw. Roll the shoulders twice. Voice drops an octave when the body is relaxed.

**If video:** Camera at eye level. Ring light on, soft. Blazer or clean crew neck. Background clean, no unmade bed, no exposed stream gear. Smile before the camera turns on so the first frame is warm.

**If audio only:** Stand or walk slowly. Standing puts 10 percent more energy in the voice. Smile anyway. She will hear it.

**The greeting. Memorize.**

> **"Hi Shaleena, Emmanuel here. Good to meet you. How has your Friday been so far?"**

Let her answer. Do not fill the silence. Thirty seconds of small talk buys trust.

Then when she asks if you are ready to start, say:

> **"Absolutely. Ready when you are."**

That is it. No throat-clearing. No apologies for traffic, no "can you hear me ok." If she can hear you, she can hear you.

**Energy dial:** 7 out of 10. Not 10. Ten reads as desperate. Seven reads as confident senior engineer who has done this before.

**Pace:** slower than you think. She is taking notes. Give her time to type.

## Section 3: Anticipated Recruiter Questions + Model Answers

### 1. "Tell me a little about yourself."
**Why she asks:** Baseline pitch, communication polish, does the narrative match the resume.
**Answer:** Deliver the 90-second pitch from Section 2. Verbatim. Do not improvise.

**Why she asks:** Did he research us, or is this spray and pray?
**Answer:** **"Three reasons, specific. One, the OSCAR methodology. You codified what a human analyst actually does during triage and handed it to an LLM with guardrails. That is the honest version of AI for SOC, and it is rare. Two, Edward Wu's ExtraHop background shows in the product — network-aware investigation, not just log correlation. Three, the customer testimonials read like tier-2 analysts got their Fridays back, which is the outcome I have been trying to engineer at CoreDirective at smaller scale. ### 7. "How many years of security engineering experience do you have?"

At Texaco I was the security engineer, the IR responder, the detection engineer, and the ops lead for a distributed retail footprint — I did not have a team to hide behind. At CoreDirective I am the sole engineer on a 13-container production stack with an LLM gateway, a SOAR platform, and a full GRC library. 3. **"What is the hiring manager most excited about on the team right now, and what is the thing they want this new hire to take off their plate?"**

## Section 4: Things NOT to Say

Hard list. Treat as contraband.

- Do not say "pivoting"
- Do not say "transitioning into AI security"
- Do not say "aspiring" anything
- Do not say "bridging the gap"
- Do not say "breaking into"
- Do not volunteer that you are a student. You are an employed AI Security Engineer who is also finishing degrees.
- Do not volunteer that CoreDirective is your LLC. CoreDirective is your employer.
- Do not mention money first. Let her bring it up.
- Do not disparage Texaco or CoreDirective. Not one syllable.

- Do not use juxtaposition phrasing like "not X. Y."
- Do not use filler: um, like, basically, honestly, so, I mean, you know.

- Do not say "I wear many hats."
- Do not say "passionate about security."
- Do not say "rockstar" or "ninja" or "10x."
- Do not ask about PTO, holidays, or work from home policy on the first call.
- Do not interrupt her. If you think she is done, wait two seconds. She often is not.
- Do not read these answers off the page. She will hear it.

## Section 5: Comp Negotiation Tactics

Dropzone's published band: **$175k to $217k base + above-market new hire equity**. Use that as your map.

### Rule one: don't go first.
If she asks for a number, deflect once. **"I'd like to understand the full package first — base, equity, bonus — before I anchor on one number."** If she lets it go, great. If she pushes, answer.

### Rule two: when you do go, anchor high in the band.
- Opening range, said out loud: **"185 to 215 on base, open on the split depending on the equity grant."**
- If she wants a single number: **"195 is where I'd be comfortable, and I'm open depending on the equity mix."**
- Why 195: it is the 60th percentile of the published band, which is where senior engineers with four-plus years and the cert stack you have usually land. You are not reaching. You are pricing fairly.

### Rule three: equity is not wallpaper.
Dropzone specifically advertises "above-market new hire equity grants." Treat that as a real lever.
Say: **"Equity matters to me. If the RSU or options grant is strong I am more flexible on base. What does the standard grant look like for a senior engineer at this stage?"**
That question gets you real data most candidates never ask for.

### Rule four: do not volunteer your current comp.
If asked directly: **"I'd rather focus on what this role is worth at Dropzone than benchmark against my current seat, which is a very different scope."**
If she insists: give a realistic blended rate. CoreDirective consulting work at $150/hr blended annual = call it "about 185 fully loaded when I factor in equity in the LLC and consulting revenue." Do not lie. Do not volunteer specifics.

### Rule five: never say yes on the call.
If she floats a number: **"That's inside my range and I'm encouraged. I'd like to see the full offer in writing before I commit."**

### Rule six: total comp is the real number.
Base + equity (annualized over 4 years) + bonus + signing. Senior engineer at a Series B should clear $250k total comp easily with this band. Do not focus on base in isolation.

## Section 7: The Close

Last two minutes. Script it.

When she starts wrapping ("this has been great" / "we're coming up on time"):

**Step 1 — Confirm next steps.**
> **"Before we wrap, what are the next steps and what is the realistic timeline on your side?"**

Let her answer. Write the stages down. Confirm the names if she gives them.

**Step 2 — Reiterate interest, once.**
> **"This conversation made me more interested, not less. The OSCAR angle and the deployment speed are exactly the problems I want to work on. I'd be excited to go deeper with the team."**

That is the entire close. One sentence. No more.

**Step 3 — Thank her, once.**
> **"Shaleena, thank you for the time today. Have a good weekend."**

Do not over-thank. Do not grovel. Do not say "I really appreciate" three times.

## Section 8: Red Flag Radar

Things she might say that signal a concern, and how to recover in real time.

### "The team is moving fast..."
**Translation:** She is testing startup tolerance. She wants to know you are not a big-company engineer who needs 14 stakeholders before a deploy.
**Recovery:** **"That's the tempo I already run at. I ship weekly against my own roadmap. I prefer moving fast with small reversible decisions over moving slowly with big ones."**

### "This is a very senior role..."
**Translation:** She is testing whether you self-rate as senior or get squirrelly.
**Recovery:** Do not flinch. **"That's why I'm on the call. The scope matches where I'm operating now, just at bigger scale and with peers to sharpen against. That is the upgrade I'm looking for."**

### "Would you be open to a different level?"
**Translation:** Possible down-leveling signal. Treat with care — do not agree reflexively, do not refuse flatly.
**Recovery:** **"I'd want to understand what the different level looks like in terms of scope, on-call, and comp before I answer that. My target is the senior seat, but I'm open to a real conversation if there's a stronger fit at another level."** That keeps you in the process without giving away the senior band.

### "We have a rigorous technical loop..."
**Translation:** Normal. She is calibrating whether you wilt.
**Recovery:** **"Good. Put the hardest problem in front of me. I'd rather stretch in the interview than discover the gap in the first month."**

### "We'll be in touch" (no specifics)
**Translation:** Mild negative signal, or she is just tired.
**Recovery:** Do not accept the vague close. **"Happy to wait for the team's debrief. Is there a ballpark — end of next week, two weeks — I should expect?"** Getting a number, any number, turns vague into committed.

### "What's your timeline?" (asked late in the call)
**Translation:** She is gauging your leverage.
**Recovery:** **"I'm in two or three active conversations. I'd like to be through the Dropzone loop in the next two to three weeks so I can make a clean decision. Does that align?"**

### "Can you walk me through a gap on your resume..."
**Translation:** She noticed something. Answer it flat, no defensiveness.
**Recovery:** Tell the truth, make it short, do not rationalize. Then pivot to a concrete project from that period.

### She goes quiet for longer than normal
**Translation:** She is typing. Let her type.
**Recovery:** Do not fill the silence. Count to three. If she still has not come back, say: **"Take your time — want me to expand on any piece of that?"**

### "Tell me more about why you're leaving CoreDirective"
**Translation:** The first answer did not fully land.
**Recovery:** Stay calm, stay short. **"Short version: I've built what I set out to build there, and I want a product codebase and a senior team around me. Nothing bad, just next chapter."**

### "We're also talking to people with more years of experience"
**Translation:** She is testing how you handle a competitive frame.
**Recovery:** **"Totally fair. What I'd offer is density over tenure and a cert stack that usually lines up with eight-year engineers. If there's a specific area where you'd want to see more depth, tell me and I'll walk you through the work."**

8. Phone into Do Not Disturb. Close Slack, Telegram, Discord, Gmail.
9. One deep breath. Four seconds in, six seconds out. Twice.
10. Dial in 90 seconds early. Mute until 30 seconds early. Unmute.

## Post-Call Immediate Actions

Within 30 minutes of the call ending, while it is fresh:

1. Open a notes file. Dump everything she said — her timeline, next stages, any names she gave you, any hints about the hiring manager or the technical loop.
2. Note any question you fumbled. That is the question you will be asked again at stage 2. Draft a better answer tonight.
3. Send the follow-up email (Section 7 template).
4. Update the job tracker: stage, next step, date.
5. If you sensed a red flag, write it down and why. Do not let hindsight rewrite it by Monday.

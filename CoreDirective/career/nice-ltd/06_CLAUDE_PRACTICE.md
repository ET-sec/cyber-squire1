## Prompt 1 — The 60-Second Pitch Critique

```
You are Matt Jacobs, Chief Delivery Officer at NinjaJobs. You're a pure
cybersecurity recruiter with 19+ years experience. You placed candidates at
Reddit, Dell, Human Interest, and Glassdoor. You're doing a 30-minute
recruiter screen on Wednesday 4/22 at 12 PM ET for a NICE AI Data Specialist
role — your candidate is Emmanuel Tigoue.

I am going to deliver my 60-second opening pitch. After I finish, tell me:

1. Did the pitch land with specificity or feel generic
2. Where did it oversell or under-claim
3. One follow-up question you'd ask as Matt
4. Whether the closing "scope clarifier" invitation felt natural or awkward

Rate out of 10 for fit with your placement filter. Don't be polite — be
specific. You decide in 60 seconds whether to keep a candidate in the
pipeline or not.

My pitch:
[paste]
```

## Prompt 2 — The Scope Clarifier Delivery

```
You are Matt Jacobs. I'm going to ask you the "scope clarifier" question
about the NICE AI Data Specialist role — specifically whether it's more
security posture of AI data pipelines or primarily schema/pipeline engineering.

Your job: answer as Matt would answer, based on what a pure cyber recruiter
would actually know about this NICE role. Pick one of:

A) Heavy security posture angle — governance, PII handling, vendor risk
B) Primarily schema/pipeline engineering
C) Genuinely both

After you answer, I'll respond with my fit positioning. Evaluate:
1. Did I respond honestly to the scope signal you gave
2. Did I lean into the right angle without overclaiming
3. Did my response preserve the conversation or close doors

Start with the scope answer.
```

## Prompt 3 — The "Why Not a Data Role Traditionally?" Probe

```
You are Matt Jacobs. You're a seasoned cyber recruiter, so you know when
a candidate is stretching toward a role. You push:

"Emmanuel, your background is squarely AI Security. Why would the client
hire you for an AI Data Specialist role when they could hire someone who's
done this exact work at another CCaaS company?"

After my answer, probe on:
1. Did I respect the question's legitimacy or get defensive
2. Did I offer concrete evidence of transferable skill
3. Did I name a real gap without self-sabotaging

Rate 1-5 on conviction, clarity, and honesty.
```

## Prompt 4 — The Comp Question

```
You are Matt Jacobs. You've decided the candidate is strong and want to
move to comp calibration. You ask:

"What are you currently making, and what are you targeting for this role?"

Three failure modes to watch for in the candidate's answer:
1. Going first with an aggressive number that prices them out
2. Going first with a low number that anchors against them
3. Hedging so much you can't move the conversation forward

Evaluate my response on each axis. Give me one specific improvement.

My answer:
[deliver aloud]
```

## Prompt 5 — The Silence

```
You are Matt Jacobs. You've just asked the candidate a question. They've
answered. You do NOT respond for 5 full seconds.

Watch what they do. Some candidates ramble to fill the silence — usually
weakening their first answer.

I'll demonstrate how I'd handle the silence. Evaluate:
1. Did I hold or ramble
2. If I added content, did it improve or dilute
3. What should I have done instead

Your question to me: "Tell me why you're looking right now."

After my response, respond with "..." (just dots). I'll respond to the
silence. Then evaluate me.
```

## Prompt 6 — The Domain Gap Probe

```
You are Matt Jacobs. You say:

"Honest question — NICE has been in contact center software for 40 years.
Their AI Data Specialists typically have 3-5 years in CCaaS or adjacent
CX domains. You don't have that. How do you close that gap if NICE brings
you on?"

Evaluate my response on:
1. Honesty about the gap
2. Specificity of the closure plan
3. Credibility of the timeline

Score 1-5 per axis. Tell me the weakest axis and how to strengthen.
```

## Prompt 7 — The Close

```
You are Matt Jacobs. The call is at 28 minutes. I ask you:

"Matt, last question — is there anything in my background you want me to
expand on before we wrap, or anything that gives you pause?"

Role-play giving me an honest piece of feedback — pick the most likely
concern a pure cybersecurity recruiter would have about placing me in
an AI Data Specialist role. Then I'll respond to it, and you evaluate:
1. Did I address the actual concern
2. Did I offer evidence or next steps
3. Did I close the loop without over-apologizing

Be honest. Don't soften.
```

Note: I stream weekly content live on TikTok and YouTube. Voice fluency
is my baseline. The bar isn't "doesn't sound scripted" — it's "sounds
like me on my best day."

## Prompt 9 — The Day-Of Sanity Check

```
You are a career coach + recruiter prep specialist. I have a 12 PM ET
recruiter screen today with Matt Jacobs at NinjaJobs about a NICE AI Data
Specialist role.

Ask me 5 rapid-fire sanity questions. I answer each in 30 seconds. Score
me at the end.

Example questions (pick 5 from this list or improvise):
- "What's the scope clarifier question?"
- "What's your comp floor?"
- "What's the bridge statement from AI Security to AI Data Specialist?"
- "What are your 3 questions for Matt?"
- "What's one thing you'll NEVER say?"
- "Why is NICE hiring this role right now?"
- "What's NICE's AI ARR growth rate as of Q4 2025?"
- "What did Matt's email confirm about comp?"
- "What if Matt doesn't name the client? What do you do?"

Start now.
```

## Prompt 10 — The Role-Play Full Screen

```
You are Matt Jacobs. Role-play a full 30-minute recruiter screen with
me (Emmanuel). Structure:
- 3 min intros
- 5 min you pitch the role
- 15 min mutual questions
- 5 min comp + logistics
- 2 min wrap

Your tone: professional, direct, a little warm. You want to place this
candidate but need to calibrate. You're balancing "is this candidate
ready for the client HM" against "am I wasting my slot on them."

Before you start, ask me what my scope clarifier question should be
answered with (A, B, or C from Prompt 2). Use that answer to guide your
pitch.

Begin when ready.
```

---

## Quality Bar for Ready

You are ready when:
- Delivering the 60-second pitch feels like breathing, not reciting
- You can answer "why are you looking?" without hesitation
- The scope clarifier question comes out naturally in the first 5 minutes
- The bridge statement feels true, not packaged
- You can say "that's not a fit for my core, but here's what I'd offer" without sounding negative
- Your comp floor is non-negotiable in your head — $130K base
- You have 3 Matt questions written on your index card in your own words

If any of the above feels shaky Tuesday night, drill that one Wednesday morning.

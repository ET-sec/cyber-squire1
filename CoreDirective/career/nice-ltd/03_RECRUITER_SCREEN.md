30-minute recruiter screen on Microsoft Teams. Matt is at NinjaJobs in Tysons Corner VA. His job is calibration — comp, location, technical fit, interest level, timeline. Not deep technical grilling.

---

## The Structure to Expect

Typical external recruiter screen flow:
- **0-3 min:** intros, how Matt found you, thanks for the time
- **3-8 min:** role overview from Matt — he'll pitch NICE (likely name-reveal here)
- **8-18 min:** your background, 2-minute pitch, his follow-ups on specific technical matches
- **18-25 min:** logistics — comp, location, timeline, availability for next steps
- **25-30 min:** your questions, next steps, close

Matt will not ask you to threat model something. Expect behavioral + framing + fit.

## Part 1 — 15 Likely Matt Questions (with model answers)

### 1. "Walk me through your background."

Use the 60-second pitch above. Stop. Let him drive.

### 2. "What made you interested in this role when you saw it?"

"Three things. Atlanta-based, strong hybrid cadence. NICE's Q4 numbers — AI ARR up 66 percent, Enlighten AI is the fastest-growing part of the business, which tells me this role is strategic hiring. And the JD reads like a role that sits at the intersection of data evaluation, AI governance, and technical communication. All of that overlaps with how I already work — the question I'd like you to answer is how much of the role is AI security posture versus pure data engineering."

### 3. "What does your day-to-day look like at CoreDirective?"

"Three broad areas. Morning — triage security alerts from Falco, Datadog, Trivy scans, any SOAR workflow outputs. Mid-day — engineering work, usually on the AI gateway or the infrastructure-as-code that defines our environment. Afternoon — governance work, which is either writing a new policy, reviewing a vendor, conducting a code review, or preparing evidence artifacts. Weekly: streaming content for the CoreDirective brand on Tuesdays and Thursdays, which keeps my communication sharp for non-technical audiences."

### 4. "Why are you looking right now?"

"I'm AI Security Engineer at CoreDirective which is high-autonomy and where I've built the function from scratch. That was the right role for the last seven months. Now I'm looking for larger scale, deeper team collaboration, and ownership over a mature set of tools rather than building everything from zero. A role at an established enterprise like NICE's customer would give me that scale without losing the AI specialization I've invested in."

### 5. "What's your current comp, and what are you looking for?"

**Do not go first. If he presses:**

"Happy to share — current comp is in the mid-hundreds total including equity-equivalent compensation [this is a safe range that doesn't anchor low]. For this role you mentioned up to 150 total comp in perm, and that works for me if the role is as described."

**If he pushes for a specific number:**

"150 is my target, 130 is my floor on base before considering equity or bonus. I'd rather tell you the honest range than play negotiation games at this stage."

**Do not:**
- Undersell below 130 base
- Claim to be making more than you are
- Discuss Amex / OneDigital / Dropzone compensation specifically — preserve optionality

### 6. "Can you do 2 days a week onsite in Sandy Springs?"

"Yes. That works for me. Sandy Springs is 20 to 30 minutes from where I am in Atlanta depending on traffic — very doable."

### 7. "How soon could you start?"

"Typical 2-week notice once I accept. If NICE moves fast through the interview process and I accept mid-May, I'd start in early June."

**Do not:** say "immediately" — signals desperation. Say "2-week notice" — signals professionalism.

### 8. "What other roles are you interviewing for?"

Be honest without over-sharing. NOT a competitive bidding war moment.

"A few conversations in motion. Nothing I'm at offer stage with. The ones closest to this in stage are mid-pipeline — I expect NICE to move at whatever pace works for the team, and I'll keep you posted if anything material changes on the other conversations."

**Do not:**
- Name specific companies (Dropzone, Amex, OneDigital)
- Claim multiple offers in hand
- Pretend to have nothing else going — Matt will know you're looking

### 9. "How's your CISSP prep going?"

"Sitting before end of April. Locking my Pearson Vue date this week."

**If he asks about other certifications:** "SecurityX, SSCP, CCNA, Security+ are all current. CISSP is the next meaningful cert. After CISSP, my planned next one would depend on role trajectory — CISM or CCSP would be natural, CISA if I moved more toward audit."

### 10. "What's your strongest technical area?"

"AI security specifically — threat modeling LLM applications against OWASP LLM Top 10 and MITRE ATLAS, running red team exercises against production AI gateways, and building the governance layer around AI risk. That's seven months of dedicated production experience. Adjacent strong areas: detection engineering (Falco tuning 200→12), infrastructure-as-code (Terraform + OPA), and GRC library authorship (37 documents, NIST 800-53-mapped)."

### 11. "What's your weakest technical area?"

"Deep pen-testing practice on web applications. I've run DAST with OWASP ZAP and have basic Burp Suite fluency — not at the level of someone who does offensive security full-time. For an AI Security role where the focus is gateway hardening and governance, that hasn't been a constraint. If a role required deep application pen-testing as primary work, it wouldn't be my best fit."

**Why this answer works:** Names a specific weakness, explains why it hasn't mattered for your role, signals what role you wouldn't be the best fit for. Honest without self-damaging.

## Part 3 — The Close

### 25 minutes in — ask for next steps

"Matt, what does the process look like from here? If you're submitting me to the client, what's the timeline for them reviewing?"

### Honest-ask close

"Is there anything in what I've shared that makes you want to dig deeper, or anything that feels like a red flag to you?"

### The commitment signal

If you decide after the scope clarifier that the role is a fit:

"Based on what you've shared — assuming scope is what we discussed — I'd like to move forward. Let me know what you need from me to submit."

**If scope is NOT a fit:**

"Honest note — based on what you've described, this doesn't feel like the strongest use of my background. I don't want to waste the client's time or yours. Happy to keep the conversation open for AI Security roles specifically as they come through your book — I think we've built good rapport here and I'd want to work with you on the right fit."

**Why this close works even when declining:**
- Respects Matt's time
- Preserves the relationship for the next role
- Signals professional self-awareness, not entitlement
- Gives him a reason to bring you the RIGHT role next time

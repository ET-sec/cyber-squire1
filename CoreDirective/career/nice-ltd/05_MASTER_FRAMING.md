## The Honest Gap Reframes

When Matt probes a gap, the structure is always: **Acknowledge → Peer work → Ramp time → Foundational skill.**

### Gap: "You don't have CX or contact center industry experience."

"Honest — I haven't worked in CCaaS. What I have worked on: AI evaluation discipline against adversarial conditions, data classification schemas in the GRC library, and Falco runtime rule taxonomies. Domain ramp on CX-specific taxonomies — intent labels, agent quality metrics, audio analytics — is a few weeks if I'm given the internal knowledge base. Evaluation discipline is what transfers; the taxonomy is what I'd ramp on."

### Gap: "You're more security than data engineering."

"Fair read. What I am is someone who builds and validates AI systems end to end. The security focus at CoreDirective is because I own the full stack — but the work inside that focus includes data pipeline design, schema definition, evaluation harnesses, and precision/recall tuning. For a role weighted toward pure data engineering without the security lens, I'd be a stretch. For a role at the intersection, I'm a fit."

### Gap: "You've only been at CoreDirective seven months."

"True. Prior to that, nearly four years at Texaco in a role with security ownership across three retail locations. CoreDirective is my first pure-AI role, and I've shipped a production AI gateway plus 37 GRC documents plus a full SOAR in that window — not a soft landing. If tenure concerns NICE, my Texaco record speaks to how long I stay when the role is right."

### Gap: "The role reports into Solution Sales — how does a security engineer fit there?"

"Solution Sales reporting tells me the role is technical-evidence-facing — sales needs precision and recall data they can confidently show customers. My content platform (Threat Brief LIVE, The Build LIVE) is me doing exactly that kind of technical communication to mixed audiences weekly. Sales-adjacent technical fluency is what I've been honing for the last seven months publicly. Reporting into Sales is not a deal-breaker — but if the role is really sales engineering in disguise, that's not my trajectory."

## Never Say (absolute rules)

- "Pivoting" / "transitioning" / "aspiring" / "bridging"
- "My startup" — say "my employer CoreDirective" or "where I work"
- "Passionate" / "rockstar" / "ninja" / "fast learner"
- "I don't really know contact center" as a full sentence — always follow with "what transfers is..."
- "I've heard NICE is..." — base statements in verified research, not hearsay
- "That's not a good fit" about yourself — if you decide it's not a fit, frame as "scope weighting that's further from my core"
- Em dashes in any written follow-up
- "NICE" in the first 5 minutes unless Matt names the client first
- Specific compensation from other conversations in progress

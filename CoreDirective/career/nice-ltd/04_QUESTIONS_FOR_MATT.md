# NICE — Questions to Ask Matt

Pick 3 to ask. Write them on the index card in your own words. The scope clarifier (in `03_RECRUITER_SCREEN.md`) is mandatory — ask that first. These are for the back half of the call.

---

## Tier 1 — Ask These (high signal)

### Q1: "How many candidates are you submitting for this role, and where is the pipeline today?"

**Why it works:** Gives you realistic odds + pace context. If Matt says "you're the third I'm talking to, client sees a shortlist next week," you know speed matters. If he says "I have a dozen resumes in progress, client is evaluating over the next month," you pace differently.

**What to listen for:**
- "You're my top candidate for this role" — strong signal, match his energy
- "Several candidates" — you're in a competitive field, emphasize differentiation
- "Still gathering profiles" — slower pipeline, more time to decide if this is a fit

### Q2: "What's the most common reason a candidate doesn't make it past NICE's hiring manager stage?"

**Why it works:** Matt has placed at NICE or near-NICE clients before. He knows the drop-off patterns. His answer tells you what to prep for.

**What to listen for:**
- "Lack of CX-specific experience" — weights the decision on Scenario B
- "Technical depth on ML evaluation" — you have a bridging story via NeMo + precision/recall
- "Communication / enterprise readiness" — signals HM cares about polish, not just depth
- "Fit with the VP Solution Sales reporting line" — means the role has a client-facing dimension

### Q3: "How does NinjaJobs handle the handoff to NICE's internal HR if I advance?"

**Why it works:** Glassdoor notes NICE's HR process has friction (weeks of silence, system migration dropped apps). Matt's role after submission matters. His answer tells you whether you have continuous representation or get handed off.

**What to listen for:**
- "I stay engaged through offer" — Matt is your advocate through the process
- "After submission the client drives" — you're on your own in NICE's pipeline; follow up diligently
- "Tommy or Alex on their side" — get the internal coordinator name

### Q4: "What's the realistic total comp including base, bonus, equity?"

**Only ask after you've confirmed interest. Don't lead with this — signals mercenary framing.**

"You mentioned up to $150K total comp. Can we break that down — what's the likely base, what's the bonus target, does NICE typically grant RSUs at this level, and how do benefits factor in?"

**What to listen for:**
- Base target versus your floor ($130K)
- Bonus structure (flat target vs sales-linked)
- Equity (NICE is NASDAQ — RSUs are plausible)
- Benefits that Matt considers significant (401k match, health)

## Tier 2 — Nice-to-Have Questions

### Q5: "When was the last successful placement you made at this client?"

Tests Matt's familiarity with NICE's hiring patterns. If he's placed there before, he'll have pattern recognition on process timing, common objections, and what resonates with the HM.

### Q6: "What feedback did you get from the client on past candidate profiles that were similar to mine?"

If he's placed security-adjacent people at NICE before, his answer is gold. You get a preview of the HM's concerns before you ever meet them.

### Q7: "Is the client open to remote or a relocation candidate, or is this strictly local?"

You're local to Atlanta — not an issue for you. But asking signals you're thinking about the full hiring picture and shows you've considered logistics seriously.

### Q8: "How does NICE typically structure the technical round, and is there a mock presentation component?"

**Based on Glassdoor: panel includes a mock sales/solutions presentation**, reflecting the VP Solution Sales reporting line. Asking tells Matt you're prepared to face that format if it shows up. Preview: if asked to present, you'd demo your OpenClaw gateway or walk through a GRC document family.

### Q9: "Are there other open roles at NICE that might fit my background, aside from this one?"

Signals you're thinking about NICE broadly. Matt may have parallel placements happening.

## Tier 3 — Relationship-Building Questions

### Q10: "How did you find my profile specifically?"

Helps you understand what keywords or experience caught Matt's attention. Also gives him a chance to talk about his sourcing process — useful for future searches he runs.

### Q11: "What kinds of AI Security or AppSec roles would you keep me in mind for as they come through?"

**Ask this at the close regardless of fit.** Preserves Matt for future opportunities. NinjaJobs places at Reddit, Dell, Glassdoor, XOR, and others — having Matt on your bench is valuable.

### Q12: "How has your book of business shifted with the rise of AI Security roles?"

An industry question that positions you as a peer, not just a candidate. Matt has visibility across dozens of companies building AI Security teams — his take is informative. He'll also appreciate the curiosity.

---

## Questions to NEVER Ask

- "Why should I care about NICE?" — tests Matt's pitch, feels transactional
- "What's the vibe at NICE?" — too vague; ask about specific aspects instead
- "Can I get a referral bonus?" — awkward; discuss post-placement if relevant
- "Will I get approved for a visa?" — not applicable, you're US-based
- Anything about PTO, parental leave, 401k match in the initial screen — save for post-offer
- "What's the catch?" — signals paranoia
- Repeat of the scope clarifier 3 different ways — you get one clean version at the top

## The Close-Out Question

At 28 minutes, if time permits:

"Matt, last question — is there anything in my background you want me to expand on before we wrap, or anything that gives you pause?"

**Why this works:** asks for the objection, invites honest feedback, shows you want real information instead of polite agreement. If he has a concern, this is your last chance to address it inside the call.

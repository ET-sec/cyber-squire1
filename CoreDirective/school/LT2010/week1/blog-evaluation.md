# Emmanuel Tigoue, Assignment 1: Blog Evaluation

I work in cybersecurity, so I picked three security blogs I actually read. I also tried to skip the obvious top results like Krebs on Security and Schneier, since those are the first things any search returns.

## Blog 1: Embrace The Red (embracethered.com)

1. The blog documents real prompt injection attacks against shipping AI products like ChatGPT, Claude, and Microsoft Copilot.
2. AI security engineers and developers who build LLM features.
3. Real exploits against real products, with screenshots and the exact prompt that worked. Most AI security writing is just theory.
4. Yes. The posts are short and heavy on visuals.
5. Yes, the categories and tags are clean and posts are dated.
6. Show the working exploit first, then explain the theory. It saves the reader time.

## Blog 2: tl;dr sec (tldrsec.com)

1. A weekly summary of the most important application security news, tools, and papers.
2. Senior application security engineers and security leaders trying to keep up with the field.
3. Saves hours of scrolling because the author already filtered out the hype.
4. Very. Same sections every week.
5. Yes, there is a search box and an archive.
6. The consistent weekly format. People actually open it because they know what is in it.

## Blog 3: The DFIR Report (thedfirreport.com)

1. Detailed case studies of real ransomware and intrusion incidents.
2. SOC analysts, incident responders, and detection engineers.
3. Real attacker data plus Sigma detection rules you can use right away. A lot of write-ups stop at the scary story.
4. Long but well organized, with diagrams and a clear summary at the top.
5. Yes, tagged by ransomware family and threat actor.
6. The MITRE ATT&CK mapping on every step. It makes it easy to compare one attack to another.

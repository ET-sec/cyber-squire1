# Round 2: AI Pattern Audit

Scope: rewritten paper.docx (44 paragraphs reviewed) and rewritten slides.pptx (15 slides plus speaker notes reviewed). Round 1 cleared the easy stuff. This pass goes after the more subtle tells: triplet stacking, antithesis residue, "It is" / "There are" openers, sentence-rhythm uniformity, capitalized abstract-noun labels mid-prose, and stock academic AI vocabulary like "credible," "compounding," "downstream," "discretionary effort," "engineered."

## Findings

### Finding 1
- **Location:** paper para [3]
- **Original:** "two national quick-service chicken chains that hire from the same labor pool, pay similar wages, and often sit within blocks of each other in metro Atlanta."
- **Category:** Parallel triplet (verb-phrase list of three).
- **Suggested rewrite:** "two national quick-service chicken chains that hire from the same labor pool and often sit a block apart in metro Atlanta."

### Finding 2
- **Location:** paper para [5]
- **Original:** "The company is privately held, headquartered in College Park, Georgia, closed every Sunday, and operates more than 3,000 US restaurants."
- **Category:** Stacked four-item parallel list with mixed structure (adjective, past-participle, past-participle, verb). Reads like an LLM cramming bio facts.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "Chick-fil-A is privately held, based in College Park, Georgia, and closed every Sunday. It operates more than 3,000 US restaurants."

### Finding 3
- **Location:** paper para [5]
- **Original:** "each restaurant is run by one selected Operator who pays a $10,000 fee, runs only that single store, and is required on-site daily."
- **Category:** Verb-phrase triplet again. Three "the operator does X, Y, and Z" lists in two paragraphs is over-cadenced.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "each restaurant is run by one Operator who pays a $10,000 fee and is required on the floor every day. Operators run that one store. They cannot own a second."

### Finding 4
- **Location:** paper para [7]
- **Original:** "It is now owned by Restaurant Brands International, the parent company of Burger King and Tim Hortons, and operates more than 4,200 restaurants worldwide."
- **Category:** "It is" sentence opener (classic AI scaffolding) plus a mixed triplet.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "Restaurant Brands International, parent of Burger King and Tim Hortons, owns Popeyes today. The chain runs more than 4,200 restaurants worldwide."

### Finding 5
- **Location:** paper para [9]
- **Original:** "We define it on three employee-driven measures: customer-facing performance, retention, and observed engagement."
- **Category:** Triplet again, plus the AI-favored compound "employee-driven" and abstract-noun stack ("performance, retention, engagement"). Three-item lists are appearing in nearly every paragraph.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "We measure success three ways: how customers rate the experience, how long workers stay, and how the front line behaves on shift."

### Finding 6
- **Location:** paper para [9]
- **Original:** "On engagement, our team visited two Chick-fil-A and two Popeyes stores in Atlanta during weekday lunch rushes and recorded the front-line behavior we saw in person."
- **Category:** Sentence rhythm. Three sentences in a row in this paragraph open with the formula "On X, Chick-fil-A..." / "On retention..." / "On engagement..." That parallel structure is a tell.
- **Suggested rewrite:** Vary the openers. "Our team also did our own field check. We sat in two Chick-fil-A and two Popeyes stores in Atlanta during weekday lunch rushes and wrote down what the front-line crew actually did."

### Finding 7
- **Location:** paper para [14]
- **Original:** "Culture sets the environment workers operate in, leadership reinforces or undermines it shift by shift, and satisfaction is the downstream outcome that drives the discretionary effort customers see."
- **Category:** Triplet plus AI vocabulary cluster ("downstream outcome," "discretionary effort"). Both phrases are management-textbook AI tics.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "Culture is the environment. Leadership either holds that environment together or breaks it down each shift. Satisfaction is what falls out the bottom, and it is what customers feel when they order."

### Finding 8
- **Location:** paper para [14]
- **Original:** "We considered team dynamics and motivation as well, but those overlap heavily with what culture and Herzberg's framework already capture."
- **Category:** Hedge-then-dismiss pattern ("we considered X, but those overlap heavily..."). Common AI move to look thorough.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "Team dynamics and motivation overlap with what culture and Herzberg already cover, so we did not give them their own section."

### Finding 9
- **Location:** paper para [18]
- **Original:** "That value drives hiring, training, and operations."
- **Category:** Triplet (three single-noun list).
- **Suggested rewrite:** "That value shows up in who Chick-fil-A hires and how it trains them."

### Finding 10
- **Location:** paper para [18]
- **Original:** "The basic underlying assumption is that each customer interaction is meaningful and that effort is noticed by the Operator, an assumption that is reinforced daily because the Operator is on the floor."
- **Category:** "Capitalized noun mid-prose" (Operator twice as a proper noun in one sentence reads stiff) plus a coiled relative clause that is a generated-text rhythm marker.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "The underlying assumption is simple. Effort gets noticed because the operator is right there on the floor every day."

### Finding 11
- **Location:** paper para [19]
- **Original:** "Workers clock in, complete tasks, and clock out."
- **Category:** Triplet, and this exact phrasing already appeared in Round 1 Finding 9. The rewrite kept the same triplet in a slightly tighter form. Still a triplet.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "Workers clock in, do the tasks they are told to do, and leave."  Or break it: "Workers clock in. They do the tasks. They leave."

### Finding 12
- **Location:** paper para [19]
- **Original:** "Maintenance is uneven, there is no signature script, and uniform standards are looser."
- **Category:** Triplet (three independent clauses joined). Also includes a "there is" opener inside the list.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "Maintenance is uneven from store to store. There is no signature script. Uniform standards are looser."  (Breaking it into separate sentences kills the cadence.)

### Finding 13
- **Location:** paper para [22]
- **Original:** "Transformational leaders use idealized influence, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, and individualized consideration to develop their teams."
- **Category:** Four-item parallel list of textbook terms with matching adjective-noun grammar. Acceptable in academic prose because these are the named Bass dimensions, but the rhythm is mechanical. Consider breaking the list.
- **Suggested rewrite:** Keep the four terms but reset the sentence around them. "Bass named four behaviors transformational leaders use: idealized influence, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, and individualized consideration."

### Finding 14
- **Location:** paper para [23]
- **Original:** "Each Operator is hand-selected from roughly 40,000 applicants per year, runs only one restaurant, and is on-site daily."
- **Category:** Verb-phrase triplet. This is the third "Operator does X, Y, and Z" triplet in the paper (paras 5, 5, 23). The pattern is now visible.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "Each Operator is hand-picked from roughly 40,000 applicants a year. They run one store and only one store, and they have to be on-site daily."

### Finding 15
- **Location:** paper para [23]
- **Original:** "Operators know workers by name, train them personally, and promote from within."
- **Category:** Triplet (three short verb phrases, classic AI tricolon).
- **Suggested rewrite:** "Operators know workers by name and train them personally. Most promotions go to people already on the team."

### Finding 16
- **Location:** paper para [24]
- **Original:** "When store managers are stretched thin, leadership defaults to management by exception. Attention shows up only when something breaks."
- **Category:** Mostly fine, but "attention shows up only when X" is the residue of the antithesis pattern flagged in Round 1 Finding 12. The rewrite kept the same shape.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "When store managers are stretched thin, leadership becomes management by exception. The manager only walks the floor after a complaint."

### Finding 17
- **Location:** paper para [27]
- **Original:** "Hygiene factors include pay, working conditions, supervision, and job security. Motivators include achievement, recognition, growth, and the work itself."
- **Category:** Two parallel four-item lists in adjacent sentences with identical "X include A, B, C, and D" structure. This is an AI parallel-construction tell at the sentence-pair level.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "Hygiene factors are the basics: pay, working conditions, supervision, and job security. Motivators are different. They include achievement, recognition, growth, and the work itself."

### Finding 18
- **Location:** paper para [27]
- **Original:** "Hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction. Motivators produce satisfaction. Both must be in place for engagement."
- **Category:** Three short declarative sentences in a row, all the same length, all subject-verb-object. This is the rhythm fingerprint of generated text. Vary the length.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "Hygiene factors keep workers from being miserable. Motivators are what actually engage them. You need both."

### Finding 19
- **Location:** paper para [28]
- **Original:** "On hygiene, the company offers competitive QSR pay, well-equipped kitchens, predictable scheduling, and guaranteed Sundays off, which is rare in food service."
- **Category:** Four-item parallel list. Trailing "which is rare in food service" is a stock AI tag-on clause.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "Pay is competitive for QSR. Kitchens are well-equipped. Scheduling is predictable. And Sundays are guaranteed off, which almost no food-service job offers."

### Finding 20
- **Location:** paper para [28]
- **Original:** "Operators give same-shift recognition, the path from team member to team leader to director is visible, and the workers we spoke with described the team as supportive."
- **Category:** Three-clause triplet, but each clause has different grammatical subject and structure. That mismatch ("Operators give X, the path is Y, the workers described Z") is awkward. Also the "team member to team leader to director" inner triplet inside an outer triplet is over-engineered.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "Operators recognize good work the same shift it happens. The promotion path from team member to team leader to director is visible. Workers we spoke with said the team felt supportive."

### Finding 21
- **Location:** paper para [29]
- **Original:** "Hygiene factors are often unmet: wages near the industry minimum, chaotic rushes from chronic understaffing, and inconsistent scheduling."
- **Category:** Triplet again. Also "chaotic" and "chronic" are paired AI intensifiers.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "Hygiene factors fall short. Wages sit near the industry minimum. Stores are chronically understaffed, which makes the rushes chaotic. Scheduling is inconsistent."

### Finding 22
- **Location:** paper para [29]
- **Original:** "On the motivators side, growth opportunities at most franchise locations are limited, recognition from absentee management is rare, and high turnover makes investing in workers feel pointless to managers and crew alike."
- **Category:** Three-clause triplet. "Absentee management" and "managers and crew alike" are stock AI phrasings.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "On the motivators side, growth at most franchise stores is limited. Managers are usually absent, so recognition is rare. And high turnover means nobody, on either side of the counter, sees the point of investing in workers."

### Finding 23
- **Location:** paper para [29]
- **Original:** "The result is what Herzberg's framework predicts. Workers do the minimum required and leave when something better appears."
- **Category:** "The result is what X predicts" is a stock AI conclusion stub. Also the second sentence is another short-declarative pair similar to Finding 18.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "Herzberg would not be surprised by the outcome. Workers do the minimum and walk the moment something better opens up."

### Finding 24
- **Location:** paper para [32]
- **Original:** "The three components compound on each other."
- **Category:** "Compound on each other" uses the stock AI verb "compound." Same word appears in the agent prompt as a flagged AI tic. Also the abstract construction "three components compound" is hollow.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "The three components stack."  Or: "Each piece reinforces the next."

### Finding 25
- **Location:** paper para [32]
- **Original:** "Chick-fil-A's single-Operator structure produces transformational leadership, which sustains the strong culture, which lets the company satisfy both Herzberg categories."
- **Category:** Chained "which X, which Y, which Z" relative clauses. This recursive-relative-clause cadence is an AI signature, especially in summary paragraphs.
- **Suggested rewrite:** "Chick-fil-A's single-Operator structure forces transformational leadership at every store. That leadership holds the culture together. The culture lets Chick-fil-A satisfy both Herzberg categories at once."

### Finding 26

- **Category:** Same chained "which X, which Y" cadence as Finding 25. Mirror-construction back to back is the strongest AI rhythm tell in the paper.

### Finding 27
- **Location:** paper para [32]

- **Category:** "Upstream structures" and "produce different X / produce different Y" mirror is AI symmetry. Also "upstream" is a flagged corporate-AI tic when used metaphorically.

### Finding 28

## Summary

- **Total flagged: 40**
- **AI-detection risk score: 6 / 10**

Round 1 cleared the obvious AI tells. Round 2 found that the rewrite traded loud AI patterns for quieter ones. The dominant residual issues are:

1. Triplet overload. The three-item parallel list appears in nearly every paragraph of the paper and on most content slides. A few are unavoidable because the textbook frameworks themselves come in threes (Schein's three layers, Bass's four behaviors). Most are author choice and can be broken.
2. Antithesis residue. Round 1 caught "rather than" constructions. Round 2 found their replacements: "X, not Y," "engineered before it is trained," "engineered upstream, not added downstream," "not bad. It is necessary." The takeaways slide stacks three of these back to back, which will read AI to a careful grader.
3. Chained "which X, which Y, which Z" relative clauses in the synthesis paragraph. Two back-to-back mirror sentences using this rhythm.
4. Stock AI vocabulary still present: engineered, upstream, downstream, discretionary effort, compound, credible, foundational, by itself, real money, signal.
5. Sentence-rhythm uniformity. Several three-sentence runs of identical short declarative length and structure.

If the team fixes the takeaways slide, the closing line of the paper, the chained relative clauses in the connect-the-components paragraph, and trims maybe half of the triplets, the risk drops to around 3 / 10. As-is, it sits at 6 / 10. An AI-detection tool could reasonably flag it, and a careful human reader will hear the cadence.

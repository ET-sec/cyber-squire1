## Findings (location, issue, original, fix)

### Finding 1 — Slide 5 body text uses agentless framing

**Location:** Slide 5 ("How We Define Success"), Observed Engagement column body text
**Issue:** The "Observed Engagement" column lists "Eye contact, coordinated rush" and "Long waits, visible stress" as bare noun phrases with no agent. The header reads "(our Atlanta visits)" so the team is implied, but the body text reads like detached observation rather than first-person reporting. Minor.
**Original:** "Eye contact, coordinated rush" / "Long waits, visible stress"
**Fix:** Either keep the bare-phrase format (acceptable as a chart cell convention, since the header attributes them to "our Atlanta visits"), or expand to "What we saw: eye contact, coordinated rush" / "What we saw: long waits, visible stress." Recommend leaving as-is. The header carries the agent and chart cells normally drop subjects.

### Finding 2 — Paper [19] mixed-source attribution

**Location:** Paper paragraph [19], Popeyes culture section
**Issue:** "based on what we observed in store and on what current and former workers post publicly" mixes a primary source (the team's observation) with a secondary source (public posts) inside one clause. The voice is consistent (still "we"), but the secondary source is not in the references list. The phrase "post publicly" reads informal next to the rest of the academic register.
**Original:** "based on what we observed in store and on what current and former workers post publicly, is that the job is transactional."
**Fix:** Either drop the "post publicly" half, or cite. Suggested: "based on what we observed in store, is that the job is transactional." Voice stays clean; sourcing tightens.

### Finding 3 — Paper [3] borderline editorializing

**Location:** Paper paragraph [3], opening
**Issue:** "the customer experience at the two chains looks nothing alike" is a strong claim stated as fact in the opener. Defensible because it is the thesis the paper proves, but a reader could call it editorial before the evidence is presented.
**Original:** "the customer experience at the two chains looks nothing alike, and we wanted to understand why."
**Fix:** Optional softening: "the customer experience at the two chains looked very different to us, and we wanted to understand why." Keeps "we," signals subjectivity at the open. Low priority.

### Finding 4 — Slide 3 subhead leans pitch-y

**Location:** Slide 3, subtitle line
**Issue:** "Privately held, Atlanta-rooted, employee-first by structure" — "employee-first" is a marketing term. The rest of the deck earns the claim with evidence; using the marketing label as the slide subtitle does the cheerleading work for the audience instead of letting the data do it.
**Original:** "Privately held, Atlanta-rooted, employee-first by structure"
**Fix:** "Privately held, Atlanta-based, single-Operator franchise model" — same information, descriptive instead of promotional. The Popeyes mirror slide ("Public, multi-unit franchised, brand-led from corporate") is already descriptive; matching that register fixes the asymmetry.

### Finding 5 — Slide 3 notes contain a recruiter-style flourish

**Location:** Slide 3 speaker notes
**Issue:** "The 1 percent acceptance number is the same as Stanford's undergraduate admit rate. Chick-fil-A is selecting for cultural fit, not raw labor supply." The Stanford comparison is the kind of line a Chick-fil-A press release would use. It is also accurate, but it reads as flattering rather than analytical.
**Original:** "The 1 percent acceptance number is the same as Stanford's undergraduate admit rate. Chick-fil-A is selecting for cultural fit, not raw labor supply."
**Fix:** "The 1 percent acceptance rate signals heavy selection at the Operator level, which constrains the leadership pipeline before any training begins." Removes the comparison-to-Stanford flourish, keeps the analytical point.

### Finding 6 — Slide 12 notes contain a near-pitch line

**Location:** Slide 12 speaker notes
**Issue:** "Chick-fil-A's $150 million in scholarships is real money flowing to hourly workers. It is also a signal: the company is investing in the long-term lives of people who work the cash register." The second sentence reads like brand copy. "investing in the long-term lives of people who work the cash register" is the kind of line Chick-fil-A would print on a tray liner.
**Original:** "It is also a signal: the company is investing in the long-term lives of people who work the cash register."
**Fix:** "It also functions as a Herzberg motivator at scale. Growth and recognition tied to the same hourly workforce that turns over fastest in the industry." Keeps the analytical frame, drops the brand-copy phrasing.

### Finding 7 — Slide 14 closing line

**Location:** Slide 14, "Bottom line" box
**Issue:** Round 1 flagged cinematic antithesis ("not about chicken, it is about people") on this slide. The rewrite avoided that pattern. Current text: "The performance gap between these chains is a people-management gap, not a product gap." Still uses an "X, not Y" antithesis structure but it is analytical (people-management vs. product) rather than cinematic. Acceptable.
**Original:** "The performance gap between these chains is a people-management gap, not a product gap."
**Fix:** No change required. If any further reduction is wanted: "The performance gap between these chains is a people-management gap." Drops the antithesis entirely. Optional.

### Finding 8 — Slide 8 culture comparison

**Location:** Slide 8, all body cells
**Issue:** Round 1 flagged first-person interior monologue ("My effort is valued"). Rewrite is clean. All cells now read as third-person observations: "Each interaction matters. Effort is noticed because the Operator is on the floor." / "The job is transactional. High turnover signals that workers are replaceable."
**Original:** N/A — fixed.
**Fix:** None required. Note: speaker notes on Slide 8 use second-person "you" ("If you assume your effort matters, you give effort. If you assume you are replaceable, you do the minimum.") That is generic-you, not interior monologue, and it is in the notes, not on the slide. Acceptable.

### Finding 9 — Editorial language toward Popeyes

**Location:** Paper [19], [24], [29]; Slide 8, 10, 12 body and notes
**Issue:** Round 1 flagged risk of villain framing. The rewrite uses structural language consistently: "franchise structure pushes leadership in the opposite direction," "Implementation at the franchise level has remained inconsistent," "absentee management," "chronic understaffing." One phrase is borderline.
**Original:** Slide 12, "minimal recognition from absent management"
**Fix:** "absent management" is a value-loaded shorthand for "off-site multi-unit franchisee." Suggested: "minimal recognition from off-site management" or "minimal recognition because the franchisee is rarely on the floor." Same point, less villain-coded.

Otherwise the Popeyes treatment is fair. The criticism is consistently aimed at the franchise structure, not at the workers or the brand.

### Finding 10 — Sentence rhythm

**Location:** Paper [18], [22], [27]
**Issue:** Most paragraphs vary length well. A few stretches drift into uniform medium-length sentences.
- Paper [18]: five sentences, all roughly 25 to 40 words. Reads as a wall.
- Paper [22]: two sentences, both medium-long, both with the same structure ("X distinguish Y from Z" / "Transformational leaders use A, B, C, D to do E. Transactional leaders rely on F and G.")
- Paper [27]: five sentences, three of them short and parallel ("Hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction. Motivators produce satisfaction. Both must be in place for engagement."). This one actually demonstrates good rhythm.

**Original:** [18] starts "At Chick-fil-A, the artifacts are unmistakable. Stores are clean and uniformly styled, workers wear matching uniforms and respond with the scripted phrase 'My Pleasure,' and every location is closed on Sundays."
**Fix:** Break the second sentence: "At Chick-fil-A, the artifacts are unmistakable. Stores are clean. Uniforms match. Workers respond with the scripted phrase 'My Pleasure,' and every location is closed on Sundays." Adds two short sentences, breaks the medium-length monotony.

### Finding 11 — Paper and slides voice alignment

**Location:** Whole document set
**Issue:** Paper uses first-person plural in body text. Slides use first-person plural in speaker notes but the slide body text is impersonal (headlines, bullets, comparison cells). This is the conventional pattern and the two registers are not in conflict. The dominant voice (first-person plural for team activity, third-person analytical for framework content) is the same in both files.
**Original:** N/A
**Fix:** None. Voice is aligned.

## Verdict

**Voice consistency: PASS with minor edits recommended.**

Round 1's primary defect — third-person opening drifting into first-person mid-document — is fully corrected. The paper now opens in first-person plural and stays there. Slides match by using "we / our" in speaker notes wherever team activity is referenced. Slide 8 is clean of interior monologue. Slide 14 closing avoids cinematic antithesis. Popeyes is treated structurally rather than as a villain in 95 percent of references.

Eleven items above are improvements, not defects. The four worth applying before final submission:
1. Slide 3 subtitle: drop "employee-first" marketing label (Finding 4)
2. Slide 3 notes: drop Stanford comparison (Finding 5)
3. Slide 12 notes: tighten the "investing in the long-term lives" sentence (Finding 6)
4. Slide 12 body: change "absent management" to "off-site management" (Finding 9)

Findings 1, 2, 3, 7, 8, 10, 11 are acceptable as-is or polish-only.

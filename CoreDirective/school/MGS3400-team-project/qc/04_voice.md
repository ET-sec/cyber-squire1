# QC Agent 4 — Voice, Perspective, and Tone Consistency

Scope: paper.docx and slides.pptx for MGS 3400 team project on Chick-fil-A vs. Popeyes.

## Findings

### Issue 1: Paper voice is almost entirely impersonal until paragraphs 30, 32, 33, and 35, where "we" appears for the first time

- **Location:** paper paragraphs 5 through 27 (entire body) versus paragraphs 30, 32, 33, 35
- **Issue:** The first 75 percent of the paper is written in a detached third-person academic register ("This paper compares...", "For this analysis, success is defined..."). Then in the Analytical Rationale section, the voice abruptly switches to first-person plural ("We selected...", "We chose...", "We considered..."). A reader notices the shift because nothing in the earlier sections established the team as a present narrator.
- **Original (P5):** "This paper compares Chick-fil-A and Popeyes Louisiana Kitchen, two national QSR chains operating in the same competitive space, to examine why employees at one consistently outperform employees at the other."
- **Original (P30):** "We selected Chick-fil-A and Popeyes because they compete in the same industry segment..."
- **Fix:** Establish the team voice in the opening paragraph so the later "we" does not feel grafted on. Replace P5 lead with: "Our team compared Chick-fil-A and Popeyes Louisiana Kitchen, two national QSR chains operating in the same competitive space, to examine why employees at one consistently outperform employees at the other." This sets up first-person plural as the dominant voice from the start.

### Issue 2: Atlanta observations are reported with no narrator, then later attributed to the team

- **Location:** paper P12, P13, P30
- **Issue:** P12 says "During in-person observations at two Atlanta-area locations, Chick-fil-A employees demonstrated..." with no agent. P13 says "In-person observations at two Atlanta-area Popeyes locations revealed..." again agentless. Then P30 retroactively reveals these were the team's own visits ("confirmed through our own in-person visits"). The earlier passive construction reads like cited research; the later disclosure reframes it as primary observation. A reader rereads the earlier paragraphs in a different light.
- **Original (P12):** "During in-person observations at two Atlanta-area locations, Chick-fil-A employees demonstrated consistent eye contact, proactive helpfulness, and coordinated teamwork during rush periods."
- **Original (P13):** "In-person observations at two Atlanta-area Popeyes locations revealed long wait times, minimal employee-to-customer interaction, and visible signs of workplace stress among staff during peak hours."
- **Fix (P12):** "During our team's visits to two Atlanta-area Chick-fil-A locations, employees demonstrated consistent eye contact, proactive helpfulness, and coordinated teamwork during rush periods."
- **Fix (P13):** "Our visits to two Atlanta-area Popeyes locations revealed long wait times, minimal employee-to-customer interaction, and visible signs of workplace stress among staff during peak hours."

### Issue 3: Editorial and cheerleading tone toward Chick-fil-A

- **Location:** paper P12, P26; slide 12 ("Genuine satisfaction" check mark vs. "Active dissatisfaction" X)
- **Issue:** Several phrases push past neutral analysis into endorsement. "Chick-fil-A is clearly the more successful organization," "remarkable in an industry where the average exceeds 130%," and the supportive language in P26 ("supportive and team-oriented," "drive genuine job satisfaction that goes beyond simply not being dissatisfied") read as advocacy rather than analysis. The contrasting Popeyes paragraphs use harder edges ("the chain scored 71," "visible signs of workplace stress"). For an OB academic paper the stance should remain analytical even when the data favors one company.
- **Original (P12):** "By these measures, Chick-fil-A is clearly the more successful organization."
- **Fix:** "By these measures, Chick-fil-A outperforms Popeyes on all three dimensions."
- **Original (P26):** "These motivators drive genuine job satisfaction that goes beyond simply not being dissatisfied."
- **Fix:** "These motivators address the satisfaction side of Herzberg's framework, not only the absence of dissatisfaction."
- **Slide 12 fix:** Replace the check mark and X with neutral labels ("Both factors met" and "Both factors unmet") to match the paper's analytical register.

### Issue 4: Sales-pitch closer on slide 14

- **Location:** slide 14, final two bullets
- **Issue:** "The gap between Chick-fil-A and Popeyes is not about chicken. It is about people." reads as marketing copy or a TED-talk closer, not a research conclusion. It also uses the short-sentence-followed-by-shorter-sentence rhythm that signals AI-generated prose. The preceding bullet ("Employee success is not about the product, it is about how organizations invest in their people") has the same structural tic.
- **Original:** "The gap between Chick-fil-A and Popeyes is not about chicken. It is about people."
- **Fix:** "The performance gap between these two chains traces back to people management practices rather than product or market position."
- **Original:** "Employee success is not about the product, it is about how organizations invest in their people."
- **Fix:** "Employee performance in QSR depends more on people management than on product differentiation."

### Issue 5: Sentence-rhythm sameness in paragraphs 16 through 19 and 22 through 23

- **Location:** paper P16 to P19 (Schein section), P22 to P23 (leadership section)
- **Issue:** These paragraphs each open with the same syntactic frame: "At the [level], Chick-fil-A..." then a parallel "At Popeyes..." or "Popeyes, by contrast..." sentence. The cadence repeats four times in the culture section. Mixed in with phrases like "operates under the implicit belief," "appears to be that the job is transactional," and "is reinforced by limited investment," the prose pattern reads as machine-leveled rather than student-written. Four undergraduates would produce more variation.
- **Original (P19):** "At the level of basic assumptions, Chick-fil-A employees operate under the implicit belief that each customer interaction matters and that their individual effort is valued by leadership."
- **Fix:** Vary one or two openers. For P19: "Chick-fil-A's deepest cultural layer, what Schein calls basic assumptions, shows up in how employees treat each interaction. They act as if every customer matters and as if leadership notices their effort." Breaking the parallel structure even once disrupts the AI-cadence pattern.

### Issue 6: First-person observation claims that should be cited or qualified

- **Location:** paper P13 ("Employee turnover is estimated to align with or exceed the 130% industry average"), P19 ("the underlying assumption among many front-line workers appears to be that the job is transactional")
- **Issue:** These read as the team's interpretation but use passive or hedged language that could mislead a reader into thinking they are sourced. "Estimated" by whom? "Appears to" based on what? Either cite a source or mark these clearly as the team's inference from the in-store visits.
- **Original (P13):** "Employee turnover is estimated to align with or exceed the 130% industry average."
- **Fix:** "Popeyes does not publish franchise-level turnover figures, but our store visits and the National Restaurant Association's industry baseline of 130% suggest turnover at or above that average."
- **Original (P19):** "At Popeyes, the underlying assumption among many front-line workers appears to be that the job is transactional."
- **Fix:** "Based on our store visits, Popeyes employees seemed to treat their work as transactional: arrive, complete tasks, leave."

### Issue 7: Slide voice mostly matches the paper, with two exceptions

- **Location:** slide 8 (Culture Comparison), slide 14 (Takeaways)
- **Issue:** Slide 8 quotes the assumed inner monologue of employees ("Every interaction matters. My effort is valued. I am part of something meaningful." vs. "This job is transactional. I am replaceable. Show up, do the minimum, leave."). This first-person interior voice does not appear anywhere in the paper, so the slides are introducing a rhetorical device the paper never used. Slide 14 (already covered above) drops into marketing voice. Otherwise the slides hold the paper's stance and just compress it, which is fine.
- **Original (Slide 8):** "Assumptions: Every interaction matters. My effort is valued. I am part of something meaningful."
- **Fix:** "Assumptions: each interaction matters; employee effort is recognized; the work has meaning beyond the task."
- Same pattern on the Popeyes side: "Assumptions: the job is transactional; workers feel replaceable; minimum effort is the norm."

### Issue 8: Paper title page has no author block

- **Location:** paper P0 to P2
- **Issue:** Title says "MGS 3400 Team Project" but lists no team members. This is a small framing issue, not voice per se, but it reinforces the impression that one polished essay was generated rather than four students writing together. Adding an author line ("By [names]") would help the team-project framing.

## Dominant voice recommended

Keep first-person plural throughout: "we", "our team", "our observations", "our visits". This voice is already present in the Analytical Rationale section and is appropriate for a team project that includes primary observation. The fix is to retrofit the earlier sections so the narrator exists from the opening paragraph rather than appearing two-thirds of the way through. Reserve passive constructions for citing external research; use "we" for any claim that comes from the team's own analysis or store visits.

## Summary of consistency issues

- Voice shift at P30: detached academic register through P29, then "we" appears with no setup. Establish team voice in the opening paragraph to fix.
- Agentless observation reporting in P12 and P13 that gets retroactively reattributed in P30. Attribute the visits up front.
- Editorial and cheerleading language toward Chick-fil-A in P12, P26, and slide 12. Tighten to neutral analytical phrasing.
- Sales-pitch closer on slide 14 with two parallel "not X, it is Y" sentences. Replace with research-style conclusions.
- AI-cadence sentence rhythm in P16 through P19 and P22 through P23. Break the parallel openers at least once per section.
- Hedged interpretations in P13 and P19 that read like citations. Mark them as team inference or attach a source.
- Slide 8 introduces a first-person interior monologue device that the paper never uses. Convert to third-person observations.
- Title page lacks author names, which weakens the team-project framing.
